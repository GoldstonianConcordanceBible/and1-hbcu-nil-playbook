# Athlete Storytelling Framework

## Purpose

This framework helps HBCU athletes tell their stories with clarity, purpose, and professionalism.

A strong athlete story can support:

- NIL opportunities
- Personal branding
- Sponsor outreach
- Social media content
- Media interviews
- Alumni mentorship
- Community connection
- Financial literacy campaigns
- Life after sports

Athletes are not only stat lines.

They are people with journeys, families, values, lessons, goals, and communities.

## Core Principle

Your story is part of your value.

But your story should be told with ownership.

Do not let sponsors, media, or social platforms define you before you define yourself.

## Why Story Matters

Story helps people understand:

- Who you are
- Where you came from
- What you represent
- Why your school matters
- What your sport taught you
- What your community means to you
- What you are building beyond sports
- Why a sponsor should care
- Why younger athletes should listen

A story creates connection.

Connection creates trust.

Trust creates opportunity.

Sponsor Story Fit

A sponsor should fit the athlete’s story.

Examples:

Restaurant

Fits stories around community, game-week routine, family, local support, and campus culture.

Barber Shop or Salon

Fits stories around confidence, community conversation, game-day preparation, and local culture.

Gym or Trainer

Fits stories around discipline, work ethic, training, comeback, and performance.

Bookstore or Tutoring Business

Fits stories around student-athlete identity, academics, leadership, and future planning.

Clothing Brand

Fits stories around style, culture, identity, and confidence.

Financial Literacy Sponsor

Fits stories around growth, responsibility, ownership, and preparation.

Story Safety Checklist

Before sharing a story, ask:

* Is this my story to tell?
* Am I sharing someone else’s private information?
* Do I have permission to use photos or videos?
* Could this hurt my family, team, school, or community?
* Could this be misunderstood by sponsors?
* Am I sharing from purpose or pressure?
* Does this help my long-term brand?
* Would I be comfortable with this being public later?

Story and Financial Literacy

Athletes can connect story to money lessons.

Examples:

* My first NIL deal taught me to track income.
* My family taught me to save before spending.
* My coach taught me discipline, and budgeting is discipline too.
* NIL is not just money. It is responsibility.
* I want younger athletes to learn financial literacy early.
* The goal is not to look rich. The goal is to build responsibly.

Story and HBCU Culture

HBCU storytelling should honor:

* History
* Alumni
* Campus traditions
* Band culture
* Academic excellence
* Community support
* School pride
* Leadership
* Service
* Family connection
* Resilience

Do not reduce HBCU culture to aesthetics.

Tell the deeper story.
