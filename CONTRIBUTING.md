# Contributing to the AND1 HBCU NIL Playbook

Thank you for your interest in contributing.

This repository exists to support HBCU athletes, former athletes, mentors, sponsors, educators, creators, and community builders.

The goal is to build open-source NIL infrastructure rooted in education, financial literacy, social media strategy, and contribution-based participation.

## Contribution Philosophy

The operating principle is:

## Eat What You Kill

This means value follows contribution.

Contributors are encouraged to build, document, teach, create, edit, mentor, research, and organize.

This repo does not reward empty status.

It recognizes work.

## Ways to Contribute

You can contribute by adding or improving:

- NIL education modules
- Financial literacy lessons
- Athlete worksheets
- Sponsor templates
- Social media campaign ideas
- HBCU NIL research
- Local business outreach tools
- DAO participation models
- Compliance checklists
- Athlete storytelling frameworks
- Media kit examples
- Deal tracker templates
- Community activation ideas
- Alumni mentor structures
- Sponsor onboarding systems
- Athlete protection language
- Case study formats

## Contribution Rules

All contributions should be:

- Educational
- Respectful
- Clear
- Practical
- Compliance-aware
- Athlete-centered
- HBCU-respectful
- Free from misleading financial claims
- Free from guaranteed income claims
- Free from guaranteed token claims
- Free from exploitative sponsor language
- Useful for real athletes and real communities

## Do Not Submit

Do not submit content that includes:

- Guaranteed NIL income claims
- Guaranteed token value claims
- Investment promises
- Passive income promises
- Misleading legal advice
- Tax advice presented as professional instruction
- Predatory sponsorship language
- Exploitative athlete compensation models
- Disrespectful language toward HBCUs or athletes
- Content encouraging athletes to hide deals from schools
- Content encouraging rule violations
- Content that uses athlete images without permission
- Content that confuses education with financial advice
- Content that treats athletes as products instead of people

* Clarity
* Compliance awareness

* Athlete protection
* Financial literacy value
* Social media usefulness
* Sponsor usefulness

Contributor Lanes

Athlete Contributor

Athletes may contribute by sharing anonymized lessons, content ideas, workflow needs, NIL questions, and educational gaps.

Alumni Contributor

Alumni may contribute by creating mentorship templates, sponsor connections, business outreach scripts, and financial literacy content.

Creator Contributor

Creators may contribute by building social media templates, short-form content prompts, campaign calendars, graphics systems, and storytelling structures.

Educator Contributor

Educators may contribute by creating lessons, worksheets, quizzes, workshops, reflection prompts, and assessment tools.

Sponsor Contributor

Sponsors may contribute by sharing responsible partnership ideas, local business use cases, and community activation models.

DAO Contributor

DAO participants may contribute by improving governance documents, participation rules, contributor scorecards, campaign templates, and ecosystem documentation.

Style Guide

Use clear language.

Avoid unnecessary jargon.

Write for athletes, parents, coaches, alumni, small business owners, and community builders.

Every file should answer:

* What is this?
* Who is it for?
* Why does it matter?
* How does someone use it?
* What risks should they understand?

Compliance Language Standard

When discussing NIL, include reminders that athletes must follow school, conference, state, federal, NCAA, and platform rules.

When discussing money, remind readers that financial content is educational and not professional tax, legal, accounting, investment, or financial advice.

When discussing tokens or DAO participation, do not promise profit, yield, appreciation, passive income, or guaranteed rewards.
