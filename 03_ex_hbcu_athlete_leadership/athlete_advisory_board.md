# Athlete Advisory Board

## Purpose

The Athlete Advisory Board gives HBCU athletes a direct voice in the AND1 HBCU NIL Playbook and AND1 DAO.

The project should not only speak about athletes.

It should listen to athletes.

The board helps ensure that NIL education, financial literacy tools, sponsor templates, social media campaigns, and DAO participation models are useful, realistic, and athlete-centered.

## Core Principle

Athletes are not just users.

Athletes are stakeholders.

They should help shape the infrastructure being built for them.

## Board Mission

The Athlete Advisory Board exists to:

- Identify athlete needs
- Review educational resources
- Give feedback on NIL tools
- Improve financial literacy modules
- Evaluate social media campaigns
- Raise compliance concerns
- Protect athlete voice
- Recommend sponsor standards
- Support peer education
- Help document real athlete experiences

## Who Should Serve

The board should include a diverse group of athletes across:

- Different HBCUs
- Different sports
- Different class years
- Different majors
- Different hometowns
- Different NIL experience levels
- Different social media comfort levels
- Scholarship and walk-on experiences, where applicable
- Men’s and women’s sports
- Revenue and non-revenue sports

The goal is not only to include the loudest voices.

The goal is to include useful perspectives.

## Suggested Board Size

A starting board may include:

- 5 to 9 student-athletes
- 1 to 2 former HBCU athlete advisors
- 1 financial literacy advisor
- 1 NIL or compliance-aware advisor
- 1 DAO documentation lead

The board can grow as the project expands.

## Athlete Board Member Responsibilities

Board members may:

- Review repo files
- Test worksheets
- Provide feedback
- Identify confusing language
- Suggest missing topics
- Share athlete concerns
- Recommend social media formats
- Help design workshops
- Participate in speaker sessions
- Support peer education
- Review sponsor fit standards
- Help define what athletes actually need

## What Board Members Should Not Be Asked To Do

Board members should not be pressured to:

- Promote tokens
- Sign NIL deals
- Recruit sponsors without support
- Share private financial information
- Share contract details publicly
- Violate school rules
- Endorse products they do not believe in
- Give legal, tax, or financial advice
- Speak for their school without authorization
- Use school marks without permission

## Board Values

The board should operate with:

- Respect
- Honesty
- Confidentiality
- Athlete protection
- HBCU pride
- Financial literacy
- Practical feedback
- Compliance awareness
- Community responsibility
- Long-term thinking

## Meeting Schedule

Suggested cadence:

- Monthly board meeting
- Quarterly strategy session
- Emergency meeting if major issue arises
- Annual review of repo resources

Board meetings should be focused, respectful, and practical.

## Standard Meeting Agenda

### 1. Welcome

Review purpose of the meeting.

### 2. Athlete Feedback

Board members share what athletes are asking, missing, or struggling with.

### 3. Resource Review

Review one repo file, worksheet, template, or campaign.

### 4. Financial Literacy Check

Discuss one money topic athletes need more support on.

### 5. Social Media Check

Discuss what content format athletes are actually using.

### 6. Sponsor and NIL Issues

Discuss sponsor questions, deal concerns, or red flags.

### 7. DAO Participation

Discuss how athletes can participate without confusion or speculative claims.

### 8. Action Items

Assign next steps.

## Feedback Categories

Board feedback should be organized into:

### Education Feedback

Is the resource clear?

Is the language easy to understand?

Would athletes actually use it?

What is missing?

### Financial Literacy Feedback

Does the money guidance feel practical?

What examples would help?

What questions do athletes still have?

### Sponsor Feedback

Would this help athletes talk to sponsors?

Are the deal examples realistic?

What local sponsor types should be added?

### Social Media Feedback

Would athletes use this content plan?

What platforms matter most?

What content feels authentic?

What content feels forced?

### Compliance Feedback

Could this create confusion?

Does it remind athletes to follow school processes?

What risk language should be stronger?

### DAO Feedback

Is the DAO language clear?

Does it avoid promises of profit?

Does it explain participation well?

Would athletes understand how to contribute?

## Athlete Privacy Standard

The board should protect athlete privacy.

Do not share without permission:

- Personal financial details
- NIL payment amounts
- Contract details
- Private family matters
- Compliance issues
- Mental health concerns
- Team conflicts
- Private sponsor discussions
- Private messages
- School-sensitive information

Athlete trust matters.

## Conflict of Interest Standard

Board members should disclose if they:

- Own a business seeking athlete promotion
- Represent a sponsor
- Receive payment from a sponsor
- Have a personal relationship with a sponsor
- Have a role in a DAO or token project
- Are promoting a service to athletes
- Benefit from a proposed campaign

Disclosure does not automatically prevent participation.

It creates transparency.

## Athlete Board Review Workflow

When reviewing a repo file:

Step 1: Read the file.

Step 2: Mark confusing sections.

Step 3: Identify missing athlete questions.

Step 4: Add real-world examples.

Step 5: Check compliance reminders.

Step 6: Check financial literacy reminders.

Step 7: Check social media usefulness.

Step 8: Recommend changes.

Step 9: Submit update to repo.

Step 10: Document what changed.

## AND1 DAO Connection

The Athlete Advisory Board is a key governance and feedback layer for the AND1 DAO.

The DAO should not only measure contributor activity.

It should listen to athlete reality.

The board can help ensure that DAO participation remains:

- Educational
- Practical
- Athlete-centered
- Compliance-aware
- Free from misleading financial claims
- Useful for HBCU communities
- Connected to real NIL needs
- Grounded in financial literacy
- Built on social media systems athletes can actually use

## Eat What You Kill and Athlete Voice

Eat what you kill does not mean ignoring athlete input.

It means contributors should do real work.

Listening is part of the work.

Improving resources is part of the work.

Protecting athletes is part of the work.

Documenting athlete needs is part of the work.

## Final Word

The Athlete Advisory Board keeps the project honest.

If athletes cannot use the tools, the tools are not finished.

If athletes do not understand the language, the language must improve.

If athletes see risks, the project must listen.

The infrastructure should serve the athlete.

The athlete voice belongs at the center.
