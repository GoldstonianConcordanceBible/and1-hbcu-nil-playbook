# Founder Model

## Purpose

This document explains the leadership model for the AND1 HBCU NIL Playbook and AND1 DAO.

The project should be led by people who understand the athlete experience, the HBCU experience, and the gap between culture and infrastructure.

The preferred founder model centers former HBCU athletes, alumni, educators, coaches, creators, and community builders.

## Core Principle

Those who lived the road should help build the road.

Former HBCU athletes know:

- The discipline required
- The missed opportunities
- The cultural value
- The financial literacy gaps
- The pressure athletes face
- The importance of alumni support
- The power of community
- The difference between attention and preparation

The AND1 HBCU NIL Playbook should not be built over athletes.

It should be built with athletes and former athletes.

## Why Former HBCU Athletes Matter

Former HBCU athletes bring lived knowledge.

They understand:

- Campus culture
- Coach expectations
- Athletic schedules
- Scholarship pressure
- Family pressure
- Community pride
- Alumni networks
- Missed NIL opportunities
- Post-sports transitions
- Financial learning curves

They can translate experience into infrastructure.

## Founder Profile

A strong founder or founding contributor may be:

- A former HBCU athlete
- A current or former coach
- An HBCU alumnus
- A financial literacy educator
- A sports business professional
- A content creator
- A local business owner
- A compliance-aware advisor
- A community organizer
- A Web3 or DAO builder
- A student-athlete development advocate

The founder does not need to be famous.

The founder needs to be useful.

## Founding Values

The founding team should commit to:

- Athlete protection
- HBCU respect
- Financial literacy
- Transparent communication
- Responsible NIL education
- No guaranteed income claims
- No misleading token claims
- No exploitative sponsor behavior
- No pressure tactics
- No hidden deals
- No culture extraction
- Community-first growth

## Leadership Lanes

### Athlete Experience Lead

Focus:

- Athlete needs
- NIL readiness
- Media kits
- Sponsor fit
- Peer education
- Athlete onboarding
- Athlete feedback

Ideal background:

Former or current athlete, coach, or student-athlete development professional.

### Financial Literacy Lead

Focus:

- Budgeting
- Saving
- Banking
- Taxes awareness
- Credit basics
- Emergency fund education
- Money habit workshops

Ideal background:

Educator, accountant, financial literacy advocate, banker, business professor, or qualified professional.

### Sponsor Development Lead

Focus:

- Local business outreach
- Alumni-owned business pipelines
- Sponsor education
- Sponsorship packages
- Community partnership models
- Responsible deal structures

Ideal background:

Entrepreneur, sales professional, alumni leader, business owner, or community connector.

### Social Media Lead

Focus:

- Content systems
- Athlete storytelling
- Short-form campaigns
- Sponsor content templates
- Highlight promotion
- HBCU culture documentation
- Platform strategy

Ideal background:

Creator, marketer, videographer, student media leader, or social media manager.

### Compliance Awareness Lead

Focus:

- School NIL process education
- Reporting reminders
- Risk checklists
- Contract red flags
- Sponsor category warnings
- Athlete protection language

Ideal background:

Compliance professional, attorney, athletic department staff member, educator, or trained advisor.

### DAO Operations Lead

Focus:

- Contribution tracking
- Governance documentation
- Participation rules
- Contributor scorecards
- $GCB ecosystem connection
- Solana participation education
- DAO transparency

Ideal background:

DAO builder, Web3 educator, operations lead, technical contributor, or governance researcher.

## Founder Responsibilities

Founders should:

- Build documentation
- Recruit contributors
- Protect athlete interests
- Create educational materials
- Avoid misleading claims
- Develop sponsor pathways
- Teach financial literacy
- Support social media strategy
- Connect alumni and athletes
- Maintain compliance awareness
- Track community impact
- Document what works
- Improve the repo

## Founder Responsibilities Do Not Include

Founders should not:

- Guarantee NIL deals
- Guarantee token rewards
- Guarantee investment return
- Pressure athletes into agreements
- Use athlete images without consent
- Hide deals from schools
- Give legal advice without qualification
- Give tax advice without qualification
- Create sponsor arrangements that risk eligibility
- Treat athletes as products
- Overpromise outcomes

Founder Selection Questions

Before adding a founder or leadership contributor, ask:

* Does this person respect HBCU culture?
* Does this person protect athletes?
* Does this person understand the mission?
* Does this person avoid misleading claims?
* Does this person bring useful skills?
* Does this person have community trust?
* Does this person understand boundaries?
* Does this person support financial literacy?
* Does this person understand compliance risk?
* Does this person build more than they talk?

Eat What You Kill Leadership

In this project, leadership is not decoration.

Leadership is contribution.

Eat what you kill means:

* Build what you claim
* Document what you do
* Teach what you know
* Share useful tools
* Create real pathways
* Support real athletes
* Respect real communities
* Measure real outcomes

A founder should be an example of contribution-based participation.

Founder Risk Standards

Founders should avoid:

* Personal enrichment without transparency
* Unclear financial arrangements
* Hidden sponsor deals
* Token hype without utility
* Using athletes to promote speculative claims
* Conflicts of interest
* Misuse of athlete data
* Overstating results
* Borrowing credibility from HBCUs without permission

Public Statement Template

The AND1 HBCU NIL Playbook is designed to be led by former HBCU athletes, alumni, educators, creators, and community builders who understand the gap between culture and infrastructure. AND1 DAO Connection

DAO leadership should reflect:

* Work completed
* Athletes supported
* Tools created
* Sponsors educated
* Compliance respected
* Community built
* Financial literacy advanced
