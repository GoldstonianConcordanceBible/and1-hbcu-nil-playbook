# Saving vs Spending

## Purpose

This guide helps HBCU athletes understand the difference between saving and spending when NIL money arrives.

NIL income can create excitement.

Excitement can create fast spending.

Fast spending can create regret.

The goal is not to tell athletes never to enjoy their money.

The goal is to help athletes make decisions before the money disappears.

## Core Principle

Every NIL payment should be split before it is spent.

Before buying anything, decide:

- What goes to taxes?
- What goes to savings?
- What goes to needs?
- What goes to emergency fund?
- What goes to brand reinvestment?
- What can be spent freely?

A plan protects the payment.

## Spending Is Not Bad

Spending is normal.

Athletes may need to spend on:

- Food
- Transportation
- Phone bills
- School supplies
- Personal care
- Clothing
- Family responsibilities
- Travel
- Training
- Content production
- Brand development

The problem is not spending.

The problem is spending without awareness.

## Saving Is Protection

Saving gives the athlete options.

Savings can help with:

- Emergencies
- Travel
- School needs
- Family needs
- Offseason expenses
- Business startup costs
- Brand building
- Training
- Moving after graduation
- Future opportunities

Saving is not just about having money.

Saving is about reducing panic.

## The First 24-Hour Rule

When a NIL payment arrives, wait before spending it.

Use the first 24 hours to:

- Record the payment
- Save proof
- Set aside tax money
- Move savings first
- Review upcoming expenses
- Decide what can be spent
- Avoid impulse purchases

Fast money should not create fast decisions.

## The First Split Rule

Before spending, split the money.

Example categories:

- Taxes
- Savings
- Emergency fund
- Needs
- Brand reinvestment
- Personal spending

Example split:

NIL payment: $200

Taxes set aside: $50

Savings: $50

Needs: $40

Emergency fund: $30

Brand reinvestment: $20

Personal spending: $10

This is only an educational example.

Each athlete’s situation is different.

The principle is the same:

Split before spending.

## Needs Before Wants

Needs are required.

Wants are optional.

### Common Needs

- Food
- Transportation
- Phone
- School supplies
- Required clothing
- Basic personal care
- Medical needs
- Housing-related costs, where applicable
- Required bills

### Common Wants

- Extra sneakers
- Eating out
- Entertainment
- New electronics
- Unplanned trips
- Fashion upgrades
- Extra subscriptions
- Impulse purchases
- Items bought for social media appearance

Wants are not evil.

But wants should not destroy the plan.

## The Three Question Rule

Before spending NIL money, ask:

1. Did I track the payment?
2. Did I save or set aside something?
3. Will this purchase still feel smart next week?

If the answer is no, pause.

## Emergency Fund First

An emergency fund should be one of the first financial goals.

Start with small goals:

Emergency Fund Goal 1: $100

Emergency Fund Goal 2: $250

Emergency Fund Goal 3: $500

Emergency Fund Goal 4: $1,000

The habit matters more than the starting amount.

## Why Athletes Need Emergency Funds

Athletes may face unexpected costs such as:

- Travel changes
- Car repair
- Family emergency
- Medical expense
- Phone replacement
- Meal gap
- Moving cost
- School supply need
- Training expense
- Delayed payment

An emergency fund helps the athlete avoid panic borrowing.

## Save First, Spend Second

A powerful habit is to save before spending.

When payment arrives:

Step 1: Record it.

Step 2: Set aside tax money.

Step 3: Move savings.

Step 4: Cover needs.

Step 5: Reinvest in brand if planned.

Step 6: Spend what remains.

This order creates discipline.

## Lifestyle Inflation

Lifestyle inflation happens when spending rises as soon as income rises.

Example:

Before NIL income, an athlete spends carefully.

After NIL income, the athlete immediately upgrades food, clothes, subscriptions, trips, and entertainment.

Soon the athlete has more income but no more savings.

The goal is to improve life without losing discipline.

## Brand Reinvestment vs Impulse Spending

Some spending can help the athlete grow.

Brand reinvestment may include:

- Headshots
- Media kit design
- Camera tripod
- Microphone
- Editing software
- Website
- Business email
- Training content
- Professional clothing for appearances
- Financial literacy education
- Tax help

Impulse spending may include:

- Buying only to impress people
- Buying because money arrived
- Buying because others are watching
- Buying without checking bills
- Buying without saving first

The question is:

Does this purchase build my future or only feed the moment?

## Social Media Pressure

Athletes may feel pressure to look successful online.

This can lead to spending on:

- Outfits
- Shoes
- Food
- Travel
- Electronics
- Events
- Appearances
- Luxury signals

Social media can make people spend money to look like they have money.

Do not let the image of success steal the foundation of success.

## The Quiet Win

A quiet financial win may look like:

- Paying a bill on time
- Saving $50
- Avoiding impulse spending
- Tracking income
- Keeping receipts
- Setting aside tax money
- Saying no to a bad deal
- Building an emergency fund
- Buying something needed instead of something flashy

Not every win needs to be posted.

## Spending Reflection

After spending, answer:

What did I buy?

Was it planned?

Was it a need or want?

Did I save first?

Did I set aside tax money first?

Will this purchase still matter in one month?

Would I make the same decision again?

What did I learn?

## Red Flags

Slow down if:

- You spend money before tracking it.
- You never know where the money went.
- You spend all NIL income immediately.
- You buy things mainly to impress others.
- You do not set aside tax money.
- You have no emergency fund.
- You borrow money while spending on wants.
- You accept deals just to fund impulse spending.
- You feel broke soon after getting paid.

Spending can make money disappear.

Saving can make money work.

NIL income should not only create a moment.
