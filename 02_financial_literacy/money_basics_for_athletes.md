# Money Basics for Athletes

## Purpose

This guide introduces HBCU athletes to basic money principles before, during, and after NIL opportunities.

NIL can create income.

Income without understanding can disappear quickly.

The goal is not only to help athletes earn money.

The goal is to help athletes manage, protect, and grow their financial discipline.

## Core Principle

Getting paid is not the same as being prepared.

A prepared athlete understands:

- Income
- Expenses
- Taxes
- Saving
- Budgeting
- Banking
- Credit
- Debt
- Contracts
- Tracking money
- Long-term planning

NIL money should become a classroom.

Every deal should teach the athlete how to handle opportunity.

## What Is Income?

Income is money or value received.

For NIL, income may include:

- Cash payment
- Check payment
- Digital payment
- Product
- Gift card
- Free service
- Discount
- Affiliate commission
- Appearance fee
- Social media campaign payment
- Merchandise collaboration payment

If an athlete receives money, product, or value in exchange for NIL activity, it may need to be tracked.

## What Is an Expense?

An expense is money spent.

Athlete-related NIL expenses may include:

- Transportation
- Gas
- Parking
- Phone equipment
- Camera equipment
- Editing software
- Design tools
- Website costs
- Printing
- Business cards
- Professional photos
- Tax preparation
- Legal review
- Clothing for appearances
- Training materials
- Content production costs

Tracking expenses matters because it helps athletes understand the real value of a deal.

A $200 deal is not really $200 if the athlete spends $75 to complete it.

## The Basic Money Equation

Money discipline starts with this equation:

Income minus expenses equals what remains.

If what remains is spent immediately, the athlete may have no savings.

If what remains is tracked, saved, and planned, NIL becomes a learning tool.

## First NIL Payment Rule

When an athlete receives the first NIL payment, do not spend it immediately.

Pause first.

Ask:

- How much did I earn?
- Do I owe taxes?
- What did I spend to earn it?
- What should I save?
- What should I use for needs?
- What can I use for wants?
- How do I record this?
- What proof should I keep?

## Suggested First Payment Split

This is only an educational example, not financial advice.

An athlete may consider dividing NIL income into categories:

- Taxes
- Savings
- Needs
- Business / brand reinvestment
- Giving / family support, if desired
- Spending money

Example:

```text
NIL Payment: $500

Possible Split:
Taxes: $125
Savings: $150
Needs: $100
Brand reinvestment: $75
Spending: $50

The exact numbers depend on the athlete’s situation.

The main lesson is this:

Do not treat the full payment like spending money.

Needs vs Wants

A need is something required for basic life, school, or responsibilities.

Examples:

* Food
* Transportation
* School supplies
* Phone bill
* Basic clothing
* Housing costs, where applicable
* Medical needs
* Required fees

A want is something enjoyable but not required.

Examples:

* Extra shoes
* Entertainment
* Eating out
* New electronics
* Fashion upgrades
* Nonessential subscriptions
* Impulse purchases

There is nothing wrong with wants.

The problem is when wants consume money needed for responsibilities.

Emergency Fund

An emergency fund is money saved for unexpected needs.

Examples:

* Car repair
* Travel emergency
* Phone replacement
* Medical copay
* Family emergency
* Lost income
* Unexpected school expense

Athletes can start small.

A first goal may be:

Emergency Fund Goal 1: $250
Emergency Fund Goal 2: $500
Emergency Fund Goal 3: $1,000

The amount matters less than the habit.

Taxes

NIL income may create tax responsibilities.

Athletes should track:

* Amount earned
* Sponsor name
* Date paid
* Payment method
* Deal purpose
* Expenses
* Tax forms received
* Copies of agreements
* Screenshots of completed posts

Athletes should not assume taxes are automatically handled.

A sponsor may not withhold taxes.

That means the athlete may need to set money aside.

Banking Basics

Every athlete should understand:

* Checking account
* Savings account
* Debit card
* Direct deposit
* Account fees
* Overdraft fees
* Minimum balances
* Digital payment apps
* Bank statements
* Fraud protection

A checking account is usually for spending and bills.

A savings account is usually for holding money.

Credit Basics

Credit is the ability to borrow money with the promise to repay.

Credit can help or hurt.

Good credit habits include:

* Paying on time
* Borrowing only what can be repaid
* Keeping balances low
* Understanding interest
* Reading terms
* Avoiding unnecessary debt

Bad credit habits include:

* Missing payments
* Maxing out cards
* Borrowing for wants
* Ignoring interest
* Opening accounts without understanding them

Debt

Debt means money owed.

Debt can come from:

* Credit cards
* Loans
* Buy now, pay later services
* Personal loans
* Car loans
* Student loans
* Unpaid bills

Debt is not automatically bad, but unmanaged debt can create long-term problems.

Before borrowing, ask:

* Why am I borrowing?
* What is the interest rate?
* What is the payment?
* When is payment due?
* What happens if I miss a payment?
* Is this need or want?
* Is there a better option?

Lifestyle Inflation

Lifestyle inflation happens when spending increases as soon as income increases.

Example:

An athlete gets paid and immediately upgrades everything.

New shoes.

More eating out.

More subscriptions.

More trips.

More impulse spending.

Then the money is gone.

The goal is not to avoid enjoying life.

The goal is to avoid letting every new dollar become a new habit.

Brand Reinvestment

Some NIL money can be reinvested into the athlete’s brand.

Examples:

* Professional headshots
* Website
* Camera tripod
* Editing app
* Resume help
* Media kit design
* Business email
* Training content
* Travel to a sponsor event
* Financial literacy course
* Tax preparation help

Reinvestment should be planned, not impulsive.

Financial Questions Before Accepting a Deal

Ask:

* Is the payment worth the deliverables?
* Will I have expenses?
* Do I need to travel?
* Do I need to buy anything?
* Will I owe taxes?
* Is payment clear?
* Is the sponsor reliable?
* Can I complete this without hurting school or athletics?
* Is this a good long-term relationship?
