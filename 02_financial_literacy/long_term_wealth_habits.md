# Long-Term Wealth Habits

## Purpose

This guide helps HBCU athletes think beyond the first NIL deal.

NIL income can create short-term opportunity.

Long-term wealth habits create discipline that can last after sports.

The goal is not to make athletes financial experts overnight.

The goal is to help athletes build habits that protect their future.

## Core Principle

The first deal should not only create spending.

The first deal should create a system.

That system should include:

- Tracking income
- Saving money
- Setting aside taxes
- Avoiding unnecessary debt
- Building credit carefully
- Reinvesting in skills
- Protecting reputation
- Learning contracts
- Thinking like an owner
- Planning beyond the season

## Wealth Is Built Through Habits

Wealth is not only about one big payment.

Wealth is often built through repeated habits.

Those habits include:

- Spending less than everything earned
- Saving consistently
- Avoiding destructive debt
- Learning how money works
- Protecting income
- Building skills
- Building relationships
- Making informed decisions
- Thinking long term

NIL can become practice for adult financial life.

## Habit 1: Track Every Dollar

If money is not tracked, it becomes easy to lose.

Athletes should track:

- NIL income
- Job income
- Camp income
- Appearance income
- Product value
- Service value
- Expenses
- Taxes set aside
- Savings
- Debt payments
- Brand reinvestment

A simple tracker is better than no tracker.

## Habit 2: Save Before Spending

When money arrives, save first.

Do not wait to see what is left after spending.

Suggested order:

1. Record the income.
2. Set aside tax money if needed.
3. Move savings.
4. Cover needs.
5. Reinvest in brand if planned.
6. Spend what remains.

This habit trains discipline.

## Habit 3: Build an Emergency Fund

An emergency fund protects against unexpected problems.

Start small.

Emergency fund goals:

- First goal: $100
- Second goal: $250
- Third goal: $500
- Fourth goal: $1,000
- Long-term goal: one month of basic expenses

The amount can grow over time.

The habit matters first.

## Habit 4: Avoid Lifestyle Inflation

Lifestyle inflation happens when spending increases every time income increases.

Example:

An athlete gets NIL money and immediately upgrades food, clothes, trips, shoes, subscriptions, and entertainment.

The athlete earns more but saves nothing.

Avoid this by deciding ahead of time how much of each payment can be spent.

## Habit 5: Learn the Difference Between Assets and Liabilities

An asset is something that may help build value, income, skill, or stability.

Examples may include:

- Education
- Skills
- Savings
- Business equipment
- Professional network
- Strong content library
- Personal brand assets
- Emergency fund
- Carefully chosen investments, when appropriate and understood

A liability is something that takes money away or creates obligations.

Examples may include:

- High-interest debt
- Unnecessary subscriptions
- Overspending on wants
- Purchases made only to impress others
- Loans for things that lose value quickly

The goal is not to avoid every liability.

The goal is to understand the difference.

## Habit 6: Protect Credit

Credit can help or hurt.

Good credit habits include:

- Paying bills on time
- Keeping balances low
- Understanding interest
- Avoiding unnecessary borrowing
- Checking statements
- Asking questions before opening accounts

Bad credit habits include:

- Missing payments
- Maxing out cards
- Ignoring interest
- Borrowing for impulse purchases
- Opening accounts for status
- Making minimum payments without a plan

Credit can affect future apartments, cars, business opportunities, and financial options.

## Habit 7: Read Before Signing

Contracts can shape future income and rights.

Before signing anything, athletes should understand:

- Payment
- Deliverables
- Timeline
- Usage rights
- Image rights
- Exclusivity
- Cancellation
- Penalties
- Confidentiality
- School reporting requirements

A bad contract can hurt more than no contract.

## Habit 8: Build Skills That Outlast Sports

Athletic ability matters.

But athletes also need skills that last beyond the season.

Useful skills include:

- Public speaking
- Content creation
- Sales
- Negotiation
- Financial literacy
- Leadership
- Coaching
- Training
- Writing
- Marketing
- Event planning
- Business communication
- Community organizing
- Technology literacy

NIL should help athletes become more valuable off the field or court.

## Habit 9: Build Relationships

Relationships can become opportunities.

Athletes should build trust with:

- Coaches
- Professors
- Alumni
- Local business owners
- Teammates
- Athletic department staff
- Community leaders
- Financial educators
- Mentors
- Sponsors
- Media creators
- Youth organizations

Do not only contact people when you need something.

Build before the ask.

## Habit 10: Reinvest in Your Brand

Some NIL money can be used to improve future opportunity.

Brand reinvestment may include:

- Professional photos
- Media kit design
- Website
- Business email
- Content tools
- Editing software
- Training content
- Public speaking coaching
- Financial literacy classes
- Business registration education
- Tax preparation help
- Professional clothing for events

Reinvestment should be planned and useful.

## Habit 11: Separate Hype From Strategy

Hype is attention without structure.

Strategy is attention with a plan.

Before accepting attention, ask:

- What does this help me build?
- Does this fit my values?
- Does this improve my future?
- Does this create risk?
- Am I prepared for the opportunity?
- Do I understand the money?
- Do I understand the contract?

Do not let hype make decisions for you.

## Habit 12: Think Like an Owner

Thinking like an owner means asking:

- What am I building?
- What do I control?
- What do I own?
- What can I learn?
- What can I document?
- What can I improve?
- What can I create that lasts?
- How can this opportunity become a system?

An owner does not only chase checks.

An owner builds assets, skills, relationships, and discipline.

## Habit 13: Give With Wisdom

Some athletes may want to support family, church, community, teammates, or younger athletes.

Giving can be meaningful.

But giving should be planned.

Before giving, ask:

- Can I afford this?
- Have I saved first?
- Have I set aside tax money?
- Is this a one-time gift or repeated expectation?
- Am I giving from pressure or purpose?
- Will this hurt my responsibilities?

Generosity should not destroy stability.

## Habit 14: Create a Post-Sports Plan

Every athlete should think beyond playing.

Questions:

- What do I want to do after sports?
- What skills am I building now?
- Who can mentor me?
- What career fields interest me?
- What businesses could I start?
- What internships should I pursue?
- What certifications could help me?
- What relationships should I build?
- What can NIL teach me about business?

Sports can open doors.

Preparation helps athletes walk through them.

## Habit 15: Keep Learning

Financial literacy is not one lesson.

It is ongoing.

Athletes should continue learning about:

- Budgeting
- Taxes
- Banking
- Credit
- Contracts
- Insurance
- Entrepreneurship
- Investing basics
- Retirement basics
- Business formation
- Marketing
- Negotiation
- Digital identity
- Intellectual property

The more athletes learn, the harder they are to exploit.

## Red Flags

Slow down if:

- You spend every payment immediately.
- You do not track income.
- You ignore possible taxes.
- You borrow money for lifestyle.
- You sign contracts without reading.
- You chase deals that do not fit your values.
- You feel pressure to look rich online.
- You have no emergency fund.
- You avoid looking at your bank account.
- You do not know what you are building after sports.
