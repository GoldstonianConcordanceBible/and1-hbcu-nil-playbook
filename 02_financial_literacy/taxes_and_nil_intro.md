# Taxes and NIL Intro

## Purpose

This guide introduces HBCU athletes to basic tax awareness related to NIL income.

This is not tax advice.

It is an educational starting point.

Athletes should consult qualified tax professionals, accountants, school resources, parents or guardians where appropriate, or trusted advisors before making tax decisions.

## Core Principle

NIL income may be taxable.

That means an athlete should not assume the full payment is spending money.

When money or value is received through NIL activity, the athlete should track it carefully.

## What Counts as NIL Income?

NIL income may include:

- Cash
- Check payment
- Digital payment
- Direct deposit
- Gift cards
- Free products
- Free services
- Affiliate commissions
- Appearance fees
- Social media campaign payments
- Camp income
- Clinic income
- Merchandise collaboration income
- Sponsored content income
- Autograph signing income

If value was received in exchange for using name, image, likeness, content, appearance, endorsement, or promotion, it should be tracked.

## Common Misunderstanding

An athlete may think:

```text
I only got free food, so it does not matter.

But product, food, clothing, services, and other non-cash value may still matter for tax or reporting purposes.

Track everything.

First Rule: Save Records

For every NIL deal, save:

* Sponsor name
* Sponsor contact information
* Date of agreement
* Date of payment
* Payment amount
* Payment method
* Products or services received
* Estimated value of products or services
* Deliverables completed
* Contract or written agreement
* Receipts
* Screenshots
* Social media post links
* School reporting confirmation, if applicable

Simple NIL Tax Record

Use this format:

Notes

Taxes Are Not Always Withheld

In a regular job, taxes may be taken out before the worker receives payment.

With NIL income, taxes may not be withheld.

That means the athlete may receive the full amount but still owe taxes later.

An athlete receives $500 from a sponsor.

The sponsor does not withhold taxes.

The athlete spends all $500.

Later, the athlete may still have a tax responsibility.

The lesson:

Set money aside before spending.

Suggested Tax Set-Aside Habit

This is an educational example, not tax advice.

Some athletes may choose to set aside a portion of each NIL payment for possible taxes.

Example:

NIL payment: $400

Possible tax set-aside: $100

Remaining amount for savings, needs, reinvestment, and spending: $300

The correct amount depends on the athlete’s full tax situation.

The habit matters:

Do not spend everything before understanding obligations.

Cash Deals

If paid in cash, still track it.

Record:

* Date
* Sponsor
* Amount
* Deliverables
* Receipt or written confirmation
* Deposit record, if applicable

Cash does not mean invisible.

Cash still needs records.

Digital Payment Apps

Athletes may receive money through digital payment apps.

Track:

* App used
* Sender
* Amount
* Date
* Purpose
* Screenshot
* Notes

Do not mix personal payments and NIL payments without records.

* Quantity
* Estimated value
* Date received
* Sponsor

Tax Forms

Athletes may receive tax forms depending on the sponsor, amount, and payment method.

Possible forms may include income reporting documents.

If a tax form arrives:

* Do not ignore it.
* Save it.
* Compare it with your records.
* Ask a qualified tax professional if unsure.

Tax Red Flags

Slow down if:

* You do not know how much NIL income you earned.
* You are paid only in cash with no record.
* You spend all NIL money immediately.
* You never save for possible taxes.
* You receive tax forms and ignore them.
* You mix personal and NIL income with no notes.
* You accept product but never record its value.
* You have no copies of agreements.
* You do not know whether the school requires reporting.

Questions for a Tax Professional

Ask:

* Should I set aside money for taxes?
* How much should I set aside?
* What records should I keep?
* Which expenses should I track?
* Do product or service deals matter?
* What forms should I expect?
* Do I need to make estimated payments?
* How should I organize my records?
* What mistakes should I avoid?
