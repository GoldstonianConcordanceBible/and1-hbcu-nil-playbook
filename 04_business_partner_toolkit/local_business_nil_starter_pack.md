# Local Business NIL Starter Pack

## Purpose

This starter pack helps local businesses understand how to begin sponsoring HBCU athletes responsibly.

Many businesses want to support athletes but do not know where to start.

This guide gives a simple, practical path.

The goal is to make NIL easier, clearer, fairer, and safer for both the athlete and the business.

## Core Principle

Start small.

Be clear.

Pay fairly.

Respect the athlete.

Follow the rules.

Document the agreement.

## What Is NIL?

NIL stands for:

- Name
- Image
- Likeness

NIL allows student-athletes to receive compensation for approved activities using their identity, image, content, public presence, or influence.

Examples include:

- Social media posts
- Store visits
- Autograph sessions
- Youth camps
- Short-form videos
- Sponsored content
- Business spotlights
- Community appearances
- Brand ambassador roles

## What NIL Is Not

NIL is not:

- Paying an athlete to attend a school
- Paying an athlete to perform in a game
- Paying an athlete based on stats
- A reason to ignore school rules
- A guarantee that a post will create sales
- A way to control an athlete
- A way to use school logos without permission
- A handshake with no clarity

## Step 1: Define the Goal

Before contacting an athlete, decide what the business wants.

Possible goals:

- Promote a restaurant
- Promote a barber shop
- Promote a gym
- Promote a product
- Support HBCU athletes
- Increase campus visibility
- Build community goodwill
- Create social media content
- Support a youth event
- Launch a game-week campaign
- Highlight alumni ownership

Write the goal:

Business goal:

Why this athlete fits:

What community impact do we want?

## Step 2: Choose a Simple Deal Type

Start with a simple partnership.

Possible starter deals:

### Social Media Thank-You

The athlete posts a thank-you message and tags the business.

### Business Spotlight

The athlete visits the business, takes a photo or video, and highlights what the business does.

### Game-Week Feature

The athlete promotes the business before or after a game.

### Appearance

The athlete attends a business event, youth event, or community activation.

### Short-Form Video

The athlete creates a short video featuring the business.

### Discount Code

The business creates a student or community discount code tied to the campaign.

## Step 3: Define Deliverables

Deliverables are what the athlete agrees to do.

Examples:

- One Instagram post
- Two Instagram stories
- One TikTok video
- One store visit
- One 30-minute appearance
- One event photo
- One sponsor thank-you
- One short interview
- One community recap video

Be specific.

Do not say:

Promote us.

Say:

One Instagram post and two Instagram stories by Friday at 5 PM.

## Step 4: Define Payment

Payment should be clear.

Write:

Payment amount:

Payment method:

Payment date:

Deposit, if any:

Product or service value, if any:

Who confirms completion?

Who sends payment?

Do not make the athlete guess when or how they will be paid.

## Step 5: Discuss Usage Rights

Usage rights explain how the business can use the athlete’s name, image, likeness, photo, video, or content.

Ask:

Can the business repost the athlete’s content?

Can the business use the content in paid ads?

Can the business use the athlete’s image on the website?

Can the business print the athlete’s image?

How long can the business use the content?

Can the business edit the content?

Does the athlete approve reuse?

Keep usage rights limited and clear.

## Step 6: Respect School Rules

Athletes may need to follow school NIL policies.

The business should encourage the athlete to:

- Review school NIL policy
- Report the deal if required
- Ask compliance before using school marks
- Avoid unauthorized logos, uniforms, facilities, or team branding
- Use required disclosure language
- Save written terms

A responsible sponsor does not ask an athlete to hide a deal.

## Step 7: Put It in Writing

A simple written agreement should include:

- Business name
- Athlete name
- Date
- Deal purpose
- Deliverables
- Payment amount
- Payment method
- Payment date
- Usage rights
- Campaign dates
- Disclosure expectations
- Cancellation terms
- Contact person
- Compliance reminder

The agreement does not need to be complicated.

It needs to be clear.

## Suggested Starter Budget Ranges

Every market is different.

Businesses should consider:

- Athlete audience
- Deliverables
- Time required
- Travel required
- Usage rights
- Content quality
- Exclusivity
- Event requirements
- Campaign length

Do not assume free promotion is fair.

If a business cannot pay much, it can still be respectful by making the scope small and clear.

## Sponsor Communication Checklist

Before campaign:

- Confirm deliverables.
- Confirm payment.
- Confirm dates.
- Confirm content requirements.
- Confirm tags and hashtags.
- Confirm disclosure language.
- Confirm whether the athlete must report to school.
- Confirm usage rights.
- Confirm who approves content.
- Confirm payment timeline.

After campaign:

- Thank the athlete.
- Pay on time.
- Save content.
- Share results.
- Ask for feedback.
- Consider future partnership.

## Sponsor Red Flags to Avoid

Businesses should not:

- Pressure athletes to sign quickly
- Refuse written terms
- Delay payment
- Ask athletes to hide deals
- Ask athletes to use school marks without approval
- Ask for lifetime image rights
- Ask for account passwords
- Ask athletes to make false claims
- Expect unlimited content for one small payment
- Use token or crypto promises irresponsibly
- Treat NIL as pay-for-play

- Provide payment records
- Encourage income tracking
- Support a savings challenge
- Fund a media kit workshop
- Host a business basics session
- Teach athletes how the business operates

A deal should be a learning opportunity.
