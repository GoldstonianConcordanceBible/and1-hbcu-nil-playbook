# Community Activation Ideas

## Purpose

This document provides community activation ideas for HBCU athletes, local businesses, alumni networks, sponsors, and AND1 DAO contributors.

A community activation is more than a post.

It is a real-world campaign, event, partnership, or public action that connects athletes, sponsors, schools, alumni, and community members.

The best NIL activations create value for everyone involved.

## Core Principle

NIL should not only move products.

NIL should move community.

A strong community activation should:

- Respect the athlete
- Support the sponsor
- Serve the community
- Follow compliance rules
- Create useful content
- Teach financial literacy
- Strengthen HBCU culture
- Build long-term relationships

## What Is a Community Activation?

A community activation may include:

- Athlete appearance
- Youth clinic
- Sponsor event
- Business spotlight
- Financial literacy workshop
- Food drive
- Book drive
- Back-to-school event
- Game-week gathering
- Alumni networking event
- Campus entrepreneurship event
- Local business tour
- Social media campaign
- Community service day
- Podcast or live interview
- Short-form video series

## Activation Planning Questions

Before planning an activation, ask:

- What is the purpose?
- Who does this help?
- Which athlete is involved?
- Which sponsor is involved?
- What community is being served?
- What content will be created?
- Is payment involved?
- Are there NIL reporting requirements?
- Are school marks, uniforms, or facilities involved?
- Are minors involved?
- Are permissions needed?
- What financial literacy lesson can be included?
- What does success look like?

## Activation 1: Game-Week Local Business Spotlight

### Purpose

Help a local business connect with HBCU game-week energy.

### Best For

- Restaurants
- Barber shops
- Coffee shops
- Clothing brands
- Gyms
- Bookstores
- Alumni-owned businesses

### Athlete Role

- Visit the business
- Create a short video or photo post
- Share why the business supports HBCU athletes
- Thank the sponsor
- Promote a student or alumni offer, if applicable

### Sponsor Role

- Provide payment
- Provide campaign details
- Support content creation
- Share athlete content
- Respect usage rights
- Pay on time

### Content Ideas

- “Game-week stop at [Business Name]”
- “Local business supporting HBCU athletes”
- “Pre-game meal with [Sponsor]”
- “Fresh cut before game day”
- “Campus favorite business spotlight”

## Activation 2: Youth Skills Clinic

### Purpose

Connect HBCU athletes with younger athletes in the community.

### Best For

- Youth sports programs
- Churches
- Community centers
- Schools
- Nonprofits
- Trainers
- Local sponsors

### Athlete Role

- Teach basic skills
- Share athlete journey
- Encourage education
- Lead drills
- Take approved photos
- Post recap, if agreed

### Sponsor Role

- Provide event support
- Provide facility or supplies
- Pay athlete appearance fee
- Handle youth safety and permissions

### Financial Literacy Add-On

End the event with a short message:

“Talent creates opportunity, but discipline protects it.”

## Activation 3: Financial Literacy Night

### Purpose

Teach athletes and students basic money skills.

### Best For

- Banks
- Credit unions
- Accountants
- Alumni business owners
- Financial literacy nonprofits
- Professional service firms

### Topics

- Budgeting
- Saving
- Banking
- Credit
- Taxes awareness
- NIL income tracking
- Contract red flags

### Athlete Role

- Attend the session
- Share one money goal
- Promote the session
- Participate in discussion
- Post educational recap, if agreed

### Sponsor Role

- Provide qualified speaker
- Provide materials
- Sponsor food or space
- Support athlete education
- Avoid giving advice outside qualifications

## Risk Checklist

Before activation, confirm:

- Athlete consent
- Written terms
- Payment terms
- School NIL process
- Sponsor usage rights
- Photo/video permissions
- Youth safety rules, if minors are involved
- Platform disclosure requirements
- No unauthorized school marks
- No misleading financial claims
- No token profit promises
- No pressure on athletes
