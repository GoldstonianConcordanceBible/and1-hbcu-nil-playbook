# Sample Sponsorship Packages

## Purpose

This document provides sample NIL sponsorship packages for local businesses, alumni-owned companies, community partners, and brand sponsors interested in working with HBCU athletes.

These packages are examples.

They should be adjusted based on:

- Athlete audience
- Sport
- Schedule
- School rules
- Sponsor goals
- Deliverables
- Usage rights
- Travel
- Campaign length
- Local market
- Athlete comfort
- Compliance requirements

## Core Principle

Simple packages help sponsors start responsibly.

A clear package should explain:

- What the sponsor receives
- What the athlete does
- When payment happens
- How content can be used
- What compliance steps matter
- What success looks like

## Important Reminder

These packages are educational templates only.

They are not legal advice, financial advice, tax advice, compliance advice, or guaranteed pricing guidance.

Athletes and sponsors should review all agreements carefully and follow school, conference, state, federal, NCAA, and platform rules.

## Package 1: Community Support Package

### Best For

- Small local businesses
- First-time NIL sponsors
- Alumni-owned businesses
- Restaurants
- Barber shops
- Coffee shops
- Bookstores
- Clothing brands
- Local service providers

### Purpose

To create a simple first partnership between an HBCU athlete and a community business.

### Suggested Deliverables

- One social media post
- One story mention
- One sponsor thank-you
- One tagged photo or short clip
- One business location visit, if applicable

### Sponsor Provides

- Payment or approved product/service value
- Business information
- Social media handle
- Campaign goal
- Preferred message
- Payment confirmation
- Written agreement

### Athlete Provides

- Professional communication
- Timely content
- Accurate tagging
- Sponsor thank-you
- Compliance reporting, if required
- Screenshots or proof of completed content

### Usage Rights

Suggested basic usage:

The sponsor may repost the athlete’s content on the sponsor’s organic social media accounts for a limited period.

Avoid broad, lifetime, or paid ad usage unless clearly negotiated and fairly compensated.

### Compliance Reminder

Athlete must follow school NIL process before posting if required.

School logos, uniforms, facilities, and team marks should not be used unless approved.

## Package 2: Game-Week Package

### Best For

- Restaurants
- Gyms
- Barber shops
- Hair salons
- Clothing brands
- Training facilities
- Local retailers
- Alumni-owned businesses

### Purpose

To connect a sponsor to the excitement of game week while supporting an HBCU athlete.

### Suggested Deliverables

- One pre-game sponsor post
- Two game-week stories
- One post-game thank-you
- One short-form video
- Optional sponsor discount code
- Optional photo at sponsor location

### Sponsor Provides

- Campaign message
- Business offer or discount, if applicable
- Payment
- Preferred posting windows
- Written terms

### Athlete Provides

- Game-week content
- Sponsor mention
- Tagging
- Disclosure language, where required
- Proof of post
- Professional follow-up

### Optional Add-On

- Appearance at sponsor location
- Youth athlete message
- Community service tie-in
- Alumni event appearance
- Behind-the-scenes video

### Compliance Reminder

Game footage, uniforms, logos, facilities, and team marks may require school approval.

Athlete should confirm rules before creating content.

## Package 3: Business Spotlight Package

### Best For

- Alumni-owned businesses
- Local entrepreneurs
- Community sponsors
- Black-owned businesses
- HBCU-adjacent businesses
- Service providers

### Purpose

To help an athlete tell the story of a business that supports HBCU athletes and the community.

### Suggested Deliverables

- One short business spotlight video
- One photo post
- Two story mentions
- One owner quote or short interview
- One sponsor thank-you
- Optional student discount mention

### Sponsor Provides

- Business story
- Location access
- Owner or manager availability
- Product or service information
- Payment
- Any approved talking points

### Athlete Provides

- Short-form content
- Authentic commentary
- Respectful business representation
- Timely posting
- Sponsor tags
- Proof of content

### Content Ideas

- “Why this business supports HBCU athletes”
- “Game-week stop at [Business Name]”
- “Local business spotlight”
- “Alumni-owned business supporting the culture”
- “Community business of the week”

## Package 4: Community Appearance Package

### Best For

- Youth sports programs
- Nonprofits
- Churches
- Community events
- Camps
- Clinics
- Schools
- Local businesses with event space

### Purpose

To create in-person community impact through athlete participation.

### Suggested Deliverables

- One athlete appearance
- One social media post before or after event
- One story recap
- Photos with permission
- Short message to youth or community attendees

### Sponsor Provides

- Event details
- Time commitment
- Location
- Payment
- Transportation support, if needed
- Event expectations
- Photo/video permissions
- Written agreement

### Athlete Provides

- Attendance
- Professional conduct
- Community engagement
- Approved social media post
- Event recap, if agreed
- Compliance reporting, if required

### Important Questions

- How long is the appearance?
- What will the athlete be asked to do?
- Will photos be taken?
- Can the sponsor use the photos?
- Will minors be present?
- Are additional permissions needed?
- Is transportation covered?
- What happens if the athlete’s team schedule changes?

## Package 5: Youth Camp or Clinic Package

### Best For

- Youth sports organizations
- Trainers
- Schools
- Churches
- Community centers
- Nonprofits
- Local businesses supporting youth development

### Purpose

To allow athletes to teach, inspire, and support younger athletes.

### Suggested Deliverables

- One camp or clinic appearance
- Skills demonstration
- Short motivational talk
- Photos or recap, if approved
- One social media thank-you
- Optional autograph session

### Sponsor Provides

- Camp details
- Safety expectations
- Payment
- Schedule
- Role description
- Liability and permissions information
- Written agreement

### Athlete Provides

- Camp participation
- Positive leadership
- Age-appropriate instruction
- Professional behavior
- Sponsor thank-you
- Content, if agreed

### Safety Reminder

When events involve minors, organizers should follow appropriate safety, supervision, permission, and facility rules.

## Package 6: Financial Literacy Sponsor Package

### Best For

- Banks
- Credit unions
- Accountants
- Financial literacy educators
- Alumni business owners
- Community sponsors
- Nonprofits
- Professional service firms

### Purpose

To support athlete education beyond promotion.

### Suggested Deliverables

- Sponsor supports a financial literacy workshop
- Athlete attends or promotes the session
- Sponsor receives thank-you recognition
- Educational recap is posted
- Athletes receive budgeting or money tools

### Sponsor Provides

- Workshop support
- Qualified educator, if applicable
- Educational materials
- Payment or sponsorship support
- Clear sponsor role

### Athlete Provides

- Attendance
- Reflection post, if agreed
- Social media thank-you
- Participation in education

### Topics

- Budgeting
- Saving
- Banking
- Taxes awareness
- Credit basics
- Emergency funds
- NIL income tracking
- Contract red flags

### Compliance Reminder

Educational sponsorship should not become misleading financial advice.

Speakers should stay within qualifications.

## Package 7: Season Ambassador Package

### Best For

- Larger local businesses
- Alumni-owned companies
- Regional brands
- Training facilities
- Apparel brands
- Restaurants
- Community organizations

### Purpose

To build an ongoing relationship between a sponsor and an athlete.

### Suggested Deliverables

- Monthly social media content
- Sponsor thank-you posts
- Community appearance
- Short-form video series
- Game-week campaign
- Business spotlight
- Optional discount code
- End-of-season recap

### Sponsor Provides

- Monthly payment or clearly defined compensation
- Campaign calendar
- Product or service support, if applicable
- Usage rights agreement
- Contact person
- Written contract

### Athlete Provides

- Scheduled content
- Professional communication
- Sponsor alignment
- Campaign participation
- Content proof
- Income tracking
- Compliance reporting, if required

### Important Review Points

Season-long agreements should be reviewed carefully for:

- Exclusivity
- Usage rights
- Payment timing
- Cancellation terms
- Injury or schedule conflict clauses
- Content ownership
- School policy issues

## Package 8: Team Culture Package

### Best For

- Sponsors who want to support multiple athletes
- Alumni groups
- Local businesses
- Community partners
- Event organizers

### Purpose

To support a group of athletes while respecting individual NIL rules and school policies.

### Suggested Deliverables

- Group athlete spotlight
- Multiple individual posts
- Community event
- Sponsor thank-you campaign
- Financial literacy session
- Content recap

### Sponsor Provides

- Clear payment structure for each athlete
- Individual agreements where needed
- Campaign details
- Usage rights
- Compliance expectations

### Athlete Provides

- Individual consent
- Agreed deliverables
- Professional participation
- Content proof
- Reporting, if required

### Risk Reminder

Avoid anything that resembles pay-for-play, recruiting inducement, or compensation tied to athletic performance.

## Pricing Considerations

This repo does not set required prices.

Pricing may depend on:

- Athlete reach
- Engagement
- Time commitment
- Content quality
- Travel
- Event length
- Usage rights
- Exclusivity
- Campaign complexity
- Sponsor budget
- Local market
- Athlete reputation
- Required turnaround time

Athletes should avoid undervaluing broad usage rights or exclusivity.

Sponsors should avoid expecting unlimited content for a small payment.

## Sponsor Success Metrics

Sponsors may measure:

- Content posted
- Views
- Likes
- Comments
- Shares
- Saves
- Story views
- Discount code use
- Event attendance
- New followers
- Community feedback
- Athlete professionalism
- Relationship quality

Do not measure success only by immediate sales.

Community trust builds over time.

## Athlete Success Metrics

Athletes may measure:

- Payment received
- Income tracked
- Taxes set aside
- Deliverables completed
- Sponsor relationship maintained
- New skills learned
- Media kit improved
- Financial literacy action completed
- Future opportunity created
- Reputation protected
