# Brand Alignment Checklist

## Purpose

This checklist helps HBCU athletes, sponsors, mentors, and AND1 DAO contributors determine whether a NIL partnership fits the athlete’s values, audience, reputation, school responsibilities, and long-term goals.

Not every deal is a good deal.

Brand alignment protects the athlete, the sponsor, and the community.

## Core Principle

The right sponsor should fit the athlete’s identity.

A NIL deal should not force an athlete to become someone they are not.

Before accepting a deal, ask:

Does this partnership make sense for my name?

## Alignment Question 4: Reputation Fit

Could this deal affect the athlete’s reputation?

Questions to ask:

- Could this deal embarrass me later?
- Could this deal hurt my school reputation?
- Could this deal hurt my family trust?
- Could this deal hurt future sponsors?
- Could this deal hurt job opportunities?
- Could this deal create negative attention?
- Could the sponsor’s reputation become my problem?

Rating:

- Low risk
- Medium risk
- High risk
- Do not proceed

Notes:

## Alignment Question 5: Compliance Fit

Does this deal fit school, conference, state, federal, NCAA, and platform rules?

Questions to ask:

- Is this sponsor category allowed?
- Does the athlete need to report the deal?
- Does content need approval before posting?
- Will school logos, uniforms, facilities, or marks be used?
- Are there disclosure requirements?
- Is the deal tied to athletic performance?
- Is the deal tied to enrollment or recruiting?
- Could this be viewed as pay-for-play?
- Is there anything the athlete should ask compliance before accepting?

Rating:

- Clear
- Needs review
- High concern
- Do not proceed

Notes:

## Alignment Question 6: Financial Fit

Does the compensation fit the work?

Questions to ask:

- What is the athlete being asked to do?
- How much time will it take?
- Does travel matter?
- Are there content production costs?
- Are there usage rights?
- Is there exclusivity?
- Is the payment fair?
- When will payment happen?
- Does the athlete need to set aside tax money?
- Is product or service value being counted clearly?

Rating:

- Fair
- Needs negotiation
- Unclear
- Not fair

Notes:

## Alignment Question 7: Usage Rights Fit

Can the sponsor use the athlete’s name, image, likeness, photo, video, or content?

Questions to ask:

- Can the sponsor repost content?
- Can the sponsor use content in paid ads?
- Can the sponsor use content on its website?
- Can the sponsor print the athlete’s image?
- How long can the sponsor use the content?
- Can the sponsor edit the content?
- Can the sponsor transfer rights to another company?
- Is usage limited and fair?
- Is the athlete being paid for broader usage?

Rating:

- Limited and clear
- Needs revision
- Too broad
- Do not proceed

Notes:

## Alignment Question 8: Exclusivity Fit

Does the deal restrict future opportunities?

Questions to ask:

- Is exclusivity included?
- What category is restricted?
- How long does exclusivity last?
- Which businesses are blocked?
- Is the athlete paid enough for the restriction?
- Could this block better deals later?
- Does it conflict with team or school sponsors?

Rating:

- No exclusivity
- Reasonable exclusivity
- Needs negotiation
- Too restrictive

Notes:

## Alignment Question 9: Social Media Fit

Does this campaign fit the athlete’s content style?

Questions to ask:

- Does this feel authentic?
- Can I create this content naturally?
- Will it confuse my audience?
- Does it match my content pillars?
- Does it require claims I cannot verify?
- Does it require music, images, or footage I do not own?
- Does it require disclosure language?
- Can I complete it professionally?

Rating:

- Strong fit
- Possible fit
- Weak fit
- Not a fit

Notes:

## Alignment Question 10: Long-Term Fit

Does this deal support the athlete’s future?

Questions to ask:

- Does this help me build useful relationships?
- Does this help me learn business?
- Does this support financial literacy?
- Does this improve my brand?
- Does this align with my long-term goals?
- Does this create future opportunity?
- Does this protect my name?
- Would I be proud of this deal after graduation?

Rating:

- Strong long-term fit
- Short-term only
- Unclear
- Not worth it

## Strong Fit Signs

A strong partnership usually has:

- Clear values alignment
- Clear deliverables
- Fair payment
- Limited usage rights
- No pressure tactics
- No hidden terms
- No compliance confusion
- Sponsor respect
- Athlete comfort
- Community benefit
- Financial tracking plan

## Weak Fit Signs

A weak partnership may include:

- Forced content
- Unclear product
- Poor audience fit
- Low payment for too much work
- Broad image usage
- Exclusivity without fair value
- Pressure to sign quickly
- Compliance uncertainty
- Reputation concerns
- Misleading claims

## Decline Message Template

Use this if the deal is not a fit.

Hello [Name],

Thank you for considering me for this opportunity.

After reviewing the partnership, I do not believe it is the right fit for my brand, school responsibilities, and current NIL goals.

I appreciate your time and wish you the best with your campaign.

Respectfully,

[Athlete Name]

## Ask for Revision Template

Use this if the deal may work with changes.

Hello [Name],

Thank you for sharing the partnership details.

I am interested in exploring the opportunity, but I would like to clarify or revise a few points before moving forward:

- [Issue 1]
- [Issue 2]
- [Issue 3]

I want to make sure the agreement is clear, fair, and aligned with my school NIL responsibilities.

Thank you,

[Athlete Name]

## Ask Compliance Template

Use this when school review is needed.

Hello [Compliance Contact],

I am reviewing a potential NIL opportunity with [Business Name].

The proposed deal includes:

- [Deliverables]
- [Payment]
- [Campaign dates]
- [Use of content / possible school marks, if any]

Before moving forward, I want to confirm whether this opportunity follows school NIL policy and whether any reporting or approval is required.

Thank you,

[Athlete Name]

## AND1 DAO Connection

Brand alignment is part of responsible DAO participation.

AND1 DAO contributors can help athletes by:

- Reviewing sponsor fit
- Building alignment checklists
- Helping athletes compare deals
- Identifying reputation risks
- Creating sponsor education materials
- Supporting social media planning
- Encouraging compliance review
- Documenting common deal patterns
- Protecting athlete voice

The DAO should never pressure athletes into poor-fit deals.

Eat what you kill means contributors build value by helping athletes make better decisions.

## Final Word

A deal should fit the athlete.

The athlete should not bend their identity around every offer.

Money matters.

But reputation matters too.

Values matter.

Audience matters.

Compliance matters.

Long-term goals matter.

When the fit is wrong, walking away can be the strongest business decision.
