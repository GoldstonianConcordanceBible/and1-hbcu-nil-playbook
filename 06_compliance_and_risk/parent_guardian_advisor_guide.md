# Parent, Guardian, and Advisor Guide

## Purpose

This guide helps parents, guardians, mentors, trusted adults, and advisors support HBCU athletes as they navigate NIL opportunities.

Student-athletes may face new pressure from sponsors, social media, contracts, local businesses, alumni networks, agents, collectives, DAOs, and digital platforms.

A trusted advisor can help the athlete slow down, ask better questions, protect eligibility, and make responsible decisions.

This guide is educational only.

It is not legal, tax, financial, investment, compliance, or professional advice.

## Core Principle

Support does not mean control.

The goal is to help the athlete become informed, confident, careful, and independent.

## Why Trusted Adults Matter

NIL opportunities may involve:

- Contracts
- Payment
- Taxes
- Social media
- Image rights
- Usage rights
- Sponsor expectations
- School reporting
- Travel
- Public reputation
- Digital identity
- Token or DAO participation
- Long-term brand consequences

Many athletes are learning these topics for the first time.

A trusted adult can help protect the athlete from rushing.

## Advisor Role

A parent, guardian, mentor, or advisor can help the athlete:

- Read agreements slowly
- Ask about payment
- Understand deliverables
- Review sponsor fit
- Identify red flags
- Track income
- Save records
- Ask compliance questions
- Build a budget
- Set savings goals
- Avoid pressure tactics
- Think beyond one deal

## Advisor Boundaries

A trusted adult should not:

- Sign for the athlete unless legally authorized
- Pressure the athlete into a deal
- Take control of the athlete’s social media accounts
- Use the athlete’s image for personal benefit
- Hide deals from the school
- Make legal claims without qualification
- Make tax claims without qualification
- Make investment claims without qualification
- Promise NIL income
- Promise token rewards
- Negotiate without understanding the athlete’s wishes
- Treat the athlete as a business asset instead of a person

## First Conversation With the Athlete

Ask:

- What do you want NIL to help you build?
- What kind of brands fit your values?
- What kind of brands do you want to avoid?
- Do you know your school’s NIL reporting process?
- Do you have a media kit?
- Do you have a budget?
- Do you track income?
- Do you know what questions to ask before signing?
- Who else do you trust for advice?
- What do you want me to help with?

## NIL Deal Review Questions

Before the athlete accepts a deal, ask:

- Who is the sponsor?
- What does the sponsor sell?
- Does the sponsor fit the athlete’s values?
- What exactly must the athlete do?
- How much will the athlete be paid?
- When will payment happen?
- How will payment happen?
- Is there a written agreement?
- Can the sponsor use the athlete’s image?
- How long can the sponsor use the content?
- Is there exclusivity?
- Are school logos, uniforms, facilities, or marks involved?
- Does the athlete need to report the deal?
- Could this hurt eligibility?
- Could this hurt reputation?

## Contract Questions to Ask

When reviewing a contract, ask:

- Can the athlete explain this agreement in plain language?
- Are deliverables clear?
- Are payment terms clear?
- Is there an end date?
- Are usage rights limited?
- Are image rights too broad?
- Is there lifetime language?
- Is there exclusivity?
- Are penalties fair?
- Can the athlete cancel if needed?
- Does the agreement require confidentiality?
- Does confidentiality interfere with school reporting?
- Does the agreement mention school marks?
- Does the contract involve tokens, crypto, NFTs, or financial claims?
- Should a qualified professional review this?

## Payment Questions

Ask:

- Is the payment fair for the work?
- Will taxes need to be considered?
- Will the athlete receive cash, check, direct deposit, digital payment, product, or service?
- Is product or service value recorded?
- Are expenses involved?
- Will the athlete spend money to complete the deal?
- Will the athlete set aside part of the payment?
- Has the payment been added to a tracker?
- Was proof of payment saved?

## Financial Literacy Support

A trusted adult can help the athlete create:

- Basic budget
- Savings goal
- Emergency fund goal
- NIL income tracker
- Expense tracker
- Tax document folder
- Deal record folder
- Monthly money check-in
- Needs versus wants plan
- First payment plan

## First NIL Payment Discussion

When the athlete receives a payment, ask:

- How much did you receive?
- Did you save proof?
- Did you record it?
- Did you complete the deliverables?
- Did you report the deal if required?
- Do you need to set aside money for taxes?
- What expenses did you have?
- What amount will you save?
- What amount will you spend?
- What did this deal teach you?

## Social Media Review

Before sponsor content is posted, ask:

- Does this fit the athlete’s values?
- Is disclosure needed?
- Is the caption truthful?
- Is the sponsor tagged correctly?
- Is school approval needed?
- Are school marks visible?
- Is music or video owned or approved?
- Is anyone else in the content?
- Does the athlete have permission to include them?
- Could this post hurt reputation?
- Was proof saved after posting?

## Reputation Protection

Ask:

- Would the athlete be proud of this deal in one year?
- Would this deal make sense to a future employer?
- Would this deal confuse family, coaches, teammates, or alumni?
- Is the athlete promoting something they understand?
- Is the athlete making claims they can prove?
- Does this sponsor respect the athlete?
- Is the money worth the attachment to the athlete’s name?

## Compliance Support

A trusted adult should encourage the athlete to ask school compliance when needed.

Useful questions:

- Is this sponsor category allowed?
- Do I need to report this deal?
- Do I need approval before signing?
- Do I need approval before posting?
- Can I use my uniform?
- Can I use school logos?
- Can I film on campus?
- Can the sponsor repost my content?
- Is this disclosure language acceptable?
- Does this contract create concerns?

## Red Flags for Trusted Adults

Pause and ask for help if:

- Sponsor refuses written terms.
- Sponsor pressures athlete to sign immediately.
- Sponsor wants school logos used without approval.
- Sponsor wants lifetime image rights.
- Sponsor wants account passwords.
- Sponsor wants the athlete to hide the deal.
- Payment terms are unclear.
- Usage rights are broad.
- Exclusivity is long or unclear.
- Deal involves gambling, supplements, alcohol, nicotine, crypto, tokens, or financial claims.
- Athlete does not understand the product.
- Athlete feels uncomfortable.
- Athlete cannot explain the agreement.

## Token, DAO, and Web3 Caution

If an opportunity involves tokens, crypto, NFTs, DAOs, wallets, or digital assets, ask extra questions.

Ask:

- What is the athlete being asked to do?
- Is the athlete being paid in tokens?
- Is the athlete being asked to promote a token?
- Is anyone promising profit?
- Is anyone promising passive income?
- Is anyone promising token appreciation?
- Is anyone asking the athlete to encourage purchases?
- Does the school policy allow this activity?
- Should compliance or legal help review this?

The AND1 DAO utility is education, financial literacy, participation, social media marketing, community coordination, and contributor recognition.

It is not a promise of profit.

## Advisor Conflict of Interest

A trusted adult should disclose if they:

- Own the sponsoring business
- Receive money from the sponsor
- Represent the sponsor
- Benefit from the athlete accepting the deal
- Own tokens involved in the campaign
- Run the DAO or project involved
- Have a personal relationship with the sponsor
- Sell services to the athlete

Transparency protects trust.

## Athlete Independence

The athlete should learn to:

- Read agreements
- Ask questions
- Track money
- Communicate professionally
- Report deals when required
- Save records
- Say no
- Negotiate respectfully
- Protect their name
- Think long term

The advisor’s job is to help the athlete grow into ownership.
