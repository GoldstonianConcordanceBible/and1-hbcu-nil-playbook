# Prohibited or High-Risk Deals

## Purpose

This document helps HBCU athletes, mentors, sponsors, alumni, and AND1 DAO contributors identify NIL deal categories that may be prohibited, restricted, risky, or inappropriate.

This is not legal advice.

This is not school policy.

This is not NCAA guidance.

This is an educational risk-awareness document.

Athletes must check their school, athletic department, conference, state, federal, NCAA, and platform rules before accepting any deal.

## Core Principle

Not every sponsor is worth the risk.

Some deals may create eligibility issues, reputation problems, legal concerns, tax complications, school policy violations, or long-term brand damage.

When in doubt, pause and ask compliance.

## What Makes a Deal High-Risk?

A deal may be high-risk if it involves:

- Restricted sponsor categories
- School policy concerns
- Age-restricted products
- Legal restrictions
- Health or safety claims
- Financial claims
- Token or crypto speculation
- Gambling or betting
- Performance-based payment
- Recruiting inducements
- School logo misuse
- Lifetime image rights
- Pressure tactics
- Unclear payment
- Reputation risk

## Always Check School Policy

Schools may restrict certain categories.

Athletes should ask:

- Is this sponsor category allowed?
- Is this product allowed?
- Is this service allowed?
- Do I need approval before signing?
- Do I need approval before posting?
- Do I need to report product or service compensation?
- Can I use school marks?
- Are there conference or state restrictions?
- Could this affect eligibility?

## High-Risk Category 1: Pay-for-Play

NIL should not be payment for athletic performance.

High-risk language includes:

- Payment for points scored
- Payment for wins
- Payment for starts
- Payment for playing time
- Payment for awards
- Payment for stats
- Payment for championships
- Payment for beating a specific team
- Payment for staying on a roster

Athletes should avoid deals tied to athletic performance.

## High-Risk Category 2: Recruiting or Enrollment Inducements

NIL should not be used improperly to recruit athletes or influence enrollment decisions.

High-risk language includes:

- Payment to attend a specific school
- Payment to transfer
- Payment to remain enrolled
- Payment to recruit another athlete
- Payment promised before enrollment in a way that violates rules
- Sponsor pressure connected to school choice

If a deal mentions enrollment or recruiting, stop and ask compliance.

## High-Risk Category 3: School Logo or Facility Misuse

Using school property, marks, or media may require permission.

High-risk content includes:

- School logos
- Team uniforms
- Locker rooms
- Practice facilities
- Stadiums
- Arenas
- Mascots
- Team names
- Official game footage
- School-owned photos
- School graphics
- School slogans

Sponsors should not pressure athletes to use school identity without approval.

## High-Risk Category 4: Gambling or Sports Betting

Deals involving gambling, sports betting, fantasy sports, picks, odds, or wagering may be prohibited or restricted.

High-risk content includes:

- Betting apps
- Sportsbooks
- Picks pages
- Odds promotions
- Gambling affiliate links
- Casino promotions
- Betting advice
- Wagering contests
- Predictions tied to payment

Athletes should be extremely careful and ask compliance before any gambling-related promotion.

## High-Risk Category 5: Alcohol, Tobacco, Nicotine, or Age-Restricted Products

Deals involving age-restricted products may be prohibited, restricted, or harmful to reputation.

High-risk sponsor categories may include:

- Alcohol
- Tobacco
- Nicotine
- Vapes
- Smoking products
- Cannabis
- THC products
- Certain CBD products
- Age-restricted venues
- Adult entertainment
- Products not appropriate for the athlete’s age or audience

Athletes should ask compliance and consider reputation risk.

## High-Risk Category 6: Supplements, Performance Claims, and Health Products

Deals involving supplements, powders, pills, performance products, or health claims can create risk.

High-risk claims include:

- Guaranteed performance improvement
- Guaranteed muscle gain
- Guaranteed weight loss
- Guaranteed recovery
- Medical claims
- Injury treatment claims
- Mental health cure claims
- Unsafe training claims
- Products with unclear ingredients
- Products banned by athletic rules

Athletes should ask athletic trainers, compliance, and qualified professionals before promoting performance or health products.

## High-Risk Category 7: Financial Products and Investment Claims

Financial products can create major risk if the athlete promotes something they do not understand.

High-risk categories include:

- Investment platforms
- Trading programs
- Crypto tokens
- NFTs
- DAOs
- Forex
- Options trading
- Sports betting picks
- High-yield promises
- Credit repair
- Loan products
- Debt products
- Buy now, pay later promotions
- Financial coaching without credentials

High-risk claims include:

- Guaranteed returns
- Risk-free income
- Passive income
- Guaranteed profit
- Token will increase
- Everyone should buy
- Secret strategy
- Quick money
- Get rich
- No risk

Athletes should avoid making financial claims they cannot verify.

## High-Risk Category 8: Token, Crypto, NFT, or DAO Promotions

Token and DAO-related campaigns require extra care.

The AND1 DAO utility is education, financial literacy, participation, social media marketing, community coordination, and contributor recognition.

It is not a promise of profit.

High-risk claims include:

- Guaranteed token appreciation
- Guaranteed pump
- Guaranteed passive income
- Guaranteed NIL payouts
- Guaranteed rewards
- Guaranteed investment return
- Risk-free participation
- Buy before it goes up
- This will make you money

Athletes and contributors should avoid investment-style promotion unless properly reviewed by qualified professionals.

## High-Risk Category 9: Lifetime Image Rights

A sponsor may try to secure long-term or permanent rights to use an athlete’s image.

High-risk contract words include:

- Forever
- Perpetual
- In perpetuity
- Irrevocable
- Worldwide
- Royalty-free
- Transferable
- Assignable
- All media
- Unlimited use

Athletes should understand exactly how their image can be used and for how long.

## High-Risk Category 10: Broad Paid Advertising Rights

A sponsor may want to use the athlete’s image in ads.

This can include:

- Paid social ads
- Website ads
- Print ads
- Billboards
- Email marketing
- Retargeting ads
- Third-party ad campaigns
- Future promotions

Paid ad usage should be clearly defined and fairly compensated.

## High-Risk Category 11: Exclusivity Without Fair Compensation

Exclusivity can block future deals.

High-risk exclusivity includes:

- Long restrictions
- Broad categories
- No extra payment
- Unclear competitor definition
- Conflict with team sponsors
- Conflict with school sponsors
- Blocks local opportunities
- Continues after the deal ends

Athletes should not give away future opportunity casually.

## High-Risk Category 12: Account Access Requests

Sponsors should not control athlete accounts.

High-risk requests include:

- Passwords
- Login codes
- Account manager access
- Ability to post directly
- Ability to delete posts
- Ability to message followers
- Ability to run ads through personal accounts
- Access to private analytics without boundaries

Athletes should protect their accounts.

## High-Risk Category 13: False or Misleading Claims

Athletes should not say things that are untrue or unverifiable.

High-risk claims include:

- This product guarantees results.
- This supplement made me better.
- This service will make you money.
- This investment is safe.
- This token will rise.
- This business is approved by my school, if not true.
- Everyone should buy this.
- This is risk-free.
- This cures a condition.
- This improves performance, if unproven.

The athlete’s trust is part of their value.

Do not trade trust for a post.

## High-Risk Category 14: Deals With No Written Terms

No written terms create confusion.

High-risk signs include:

- Verbal-only payment promise
- No deliverable list
- No payment date
- No usage rights
- No campaign dates
- No contact person
- No cancellation terms
- No proof of agreement

A simple written agreement is better than memory.

## High-Risk Category 15: Pressure to Sign Immediately

Pressure is a warning sign.

High-risk phrases include:

- This offer expires right now.
- Do not ask anyone.
- You do not need compliance.
- Everybody signs this.
- We need your answer today.
- Do not overthink it.
- This is standard.
- You will miss your chance.

A responsible partner gives the athlete time to understand the deal.

## High-Risk Category 16: Deals That Require Secrecy From School

Athletes should not hide reportable NIL activity.

High-risk phrases include:

- Do not tell compliance.
- The school does not need to know.
- This is off the books.
- Just post it.
- Nobody will find out.
- We can keep this between us.

If the sponsor encourages hiding the deal, stop.

## High-Risk Category 17: Unclear Product or Business Model

If the athlete cannot explain what the sponsor does, the deal may be risky.

Ask:

- What does this business sell?
- Who owns it?
- Is it legitimate?
- Is it legal?
- Who is the customer?
- What claims are being made?
- Would I use this product?
- Would I recommend this to family?
- Could this harm my audience?

Do not promote what you do not understand.

## High-Risk Category 18: Compensation Only in Exposure

Exposure can be useful in some contexts, but it should not be used to exploit athletes.

High-risk signs include:

- Sponsor expects major deliverables for no pay
- Sponsor promises future deals but no current value
- Sponsor wants image rights without compensation
- Sponsor says exposure is payment
- Sponsor uses athlete content for business gain without fair exchange

Athletes can volunteer for causes they believe in, but they should understand the difference between service and commercial promotion.

## High-Risk Category 19: Unclear Tax or Payment Records

A deal may create tax or recordkeeping responsibilities.

High-risk signs include:

- Cash with no receipt
- Product value not recorded
- Payment from unknown person
- Digital payment with no note
- No invoice
- No agreement
- No tax form when expected
- No record of expenses

Athletes should track everything.

## High-Risk Category 20: Reputation Misalignment

Some deals may be allowed but still not wise.

Ask:

- Does this fit my values?
- Does this fit my audience?
- Would I be proud of this later?
- Could this hurt future opportunities?
- Could this hurt my family trust?
- Could this hurt my school reputation?
- Does this conflict with my faith, mission, or public identity?
- Is this worth attaching to my name?

Allowed does not always mean aligned.

## Questions Before Accepting a High-Risk Deal

Ask:

- Is this allowed by my school?
- Is this allowed by state law?
- Is this allowed by NCAA rules?
- Is this sponsor category restricted?
- Is this product appropriate?
- Is this tied to performance or enrollment?
- Are school marks involved?
- Are usage rights too broad?
- Is payment clear?
- Is there written documentation?
- Does this create tax issues?
- Does this damage my reputation?
- Do I fully understand what I am promoting?
- Should I walk away?
