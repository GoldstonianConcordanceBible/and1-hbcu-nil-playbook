# Compliance Overview

## Purpose

This document gives HBCU athletes, sponsors, mentors, alumni, and AND1 DAO contributors a basic overview of NIL compliance awareness.

This is not legal advice.

This is not school policy.

This is not NCAA guidance.

This is an educational checklist to help athletes ask better questions before accepting, posting, promoting, signing, or participating in NIL activity.

## Core Principle

No deal is worth risking eligibility.

NIL opportunity should never move faster than compliance awareness.

Before an athlete accepts a deal, they should understand the rules that apply to them.

## What Is Compliance?

Compliance means following the rules, policies, laws, and procedures that apply to NIL activity.

Those rules may come from:

- The athlete’s school
- The athletic department
- The conference
- State law
- Federal law
- NCAA rules
- Team rules
- Sponsor rules
- Platform rules
- Tax rules
- Contract terms

Rules may vary by school and location.

Athletes should not assume another athlete’s rule situation applies to them.

## Why Compliance Matters

Compliance protects:

- Eligibility
- Scholarships
- Team standing
- School relationships
- Sponsor relationships
- Athlete reputation
- Future opportunities
- Tax records
- Legal rights
- Brand trust

A deal that looks exciting can become a problem if the athlete does not understand the rules.

## School NIL Policy

Every athlete should know where to find their school’s NIL policy.

Questions:

- Where is the NIL policy posted?
- Who is the compliance contact?
- What must be reported?
- When must it be reported?
- Is approval required before signing?
- Is approval required before posting?
- Are certain sponsor categories prohibited?
- Are school logos allowed?
- Are uniforms allowed?
- Are facilities allowed?
- Are team marks allowed?
- Is game footage allowed?
- Are there rules about agents or representatives?

## Reporting Requirements

Some schools may require athletes to report NIL activity.

Athletes should ask:

- Do I report before or after signing?
- Do I report before or after payment?
- Do I report product-only deals?
- Do I report free services?
- Do I report affiliate codes?
- Do I report appearances?
- Do I report social media posts?
- What platform or form do I use?
- What documents are required?
- What happens if I forget?

## Common NIL Compliance Risks

### Risk 1: Using School Marks Without Approval

School marks may include:

- Logos
- Mascots
- Uniforms
- Team names
- Facility images
- School slogans
- Official graphics
- Game footage

Athletes should ask before using official marks in sponsor content.

### Risk 2: Sponsor Category Restrictions

Some sponsor categories may be restricted or prohibited by school or law.

Athletes should ask compliance before promoting sensitive categories.

### Risk 3: Pay-for-Play Concerns

NIL should not be payment for athletic performance.

Avoid arrangements that pay based on:

- Points scored
- Wins
- Stats
- Playing time
- Team results
- Enrolling at a school
- Staying at a school
- Recruiting another athlete

### Risk 4: Recruiting Inducement Concerns

NIL should not be used as an improper recruiting tool.

Athletes, sponsors, alumni, and contributors should avoid making promises to prospects that violate rules.

### Risk 5: Hidden Deals

Athletes should not hide NIL deals from required school reporting processes.

If a sponsor asks an athlete to keep a deal secret from compliance, that is a red flag.

### Risk 6: Unclear Contracts

Contracts with unclear payment, deliverables, usage rights, exclusivity, or cancellation terms can create risk.

### Risk 7: Social Media Disclosure Problems

Sponsored content may need clear disclosure.

Athletes should understand platform and sponsorship disclosure expectations.

### Risk 8: Token or Crypto Misrepresentation

If a campaign involves tokens, crypto, DAOs, NFTs, or digital assets, athletes should avoid misleading profit claims, guaranteed returns, or investment language.

## School Marks Checklist

Before using any school-related image or reference in sponsor content, ask:

- Am I wearing a school uniform?
- Is a school logo visible?
- Is the mascot visible?
- Is the facility visible?
- Is the team name visible?
- Is official game footage being used?
- Is school-owned media being used?
- Does the sponsor want to use school identity?
- Has compliance approved this use?
- Is written permission needed?

## Sponsor Category Checklist

Before accepting a sponsor, ask:

- Is this sponsor allowed by school policy?
- Is this sponsor allowed by state law?
- Is this sponsor appropriate for my age?
- Is this sponsor appropriate for my audience?
- Could this sponsor hurt my reputation?
- Could this sponsor create eligibility concerns?
- Does this sponsor make financial claims?
- Does this sponsor involve tokens, crypto, gambling, alcohol, supplements, or other sensitive categories?
- Should I ask compliance before proceeding?

## Contract Compliance Checklist

Before signing, ask:

- Does the contract identify the sponsor?
- Does it identify the athlete?
- Does it define deliverables?
- Does it define payment?
- Does it define dates?
- Does it define usage rights?
- Does it define exclusivity?
- Does it include school marks?
- Does it include compliance language?
- Does it require anything that violates school policy?
- Does it require the athlete to hide the agreement?
- Does it create obligations after the deal ends?

## Social Media Compliance Checklist

Before posting sponsored content, ask:

- Is this a paid partnership?
- Is disclosure needed?
- Is the sponsor tagged correctly?
- Does the caption make truthful claims?
- Am I using school marks?
- Am I using copyrighted music?
- Am I using someone else’s photo or video?
- Is anyone else visible in the content?
- Do I need permission?
- Did I report the deal if required?
- Did I save proof of posting?

## Disclosure Examples

Athletes should follow applicable rules, but possible disclosure language may include:

- Paid partnership with [Business Name]
- Sponsored by [Business Name]
- NIL partnership with [Business Name]
- Ad, where required
- Advertisement, where required
- Thank you to [Business Name] for sponsoring this NIL partnership

Disclosures should be clear and easy to see.

## Documentation Checklist

- Product or service value
- Receipts
- Screenshots
- Posted content
- Analytics, if relevant
- School reporting confirmation
- Tax documents
- Content approval messages
- Usage rights terms

## Red Flags

Slow down if:

- Sponsor says you do not need to report the deal.
- Sponsor wants school logos used without approval.
- Sponsor refuses written terms.
- Sponsor pays based on performance stats.
- Sponsor ties payment to enrollment or recruiting.
- Sponsor asks you to hide the deal.
- Sponsor wants lifetime image rights.
- Sponsor wants social media account access.
- Sponsor asks you to make claims you cannot verify.
- Sponsor promotes guaranteed token or investment returns.
- You do not understand the agreement.
