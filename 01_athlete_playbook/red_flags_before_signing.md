# Red Flags Before Signing

## Purpose

This guide helps HBCU athletes recognize warning signs before signing NIL agreements, sponsorship contracts, content deals, appearance agreements, affiliate partnerships, or DAO-related participation commitments.

A bad deal can cost more than money.

It can affect:

- Eligibility
- Reputation
- Future opportunities
- Content rights
- Image rights
- Tax responsibilities
- Sponsor relationships
- School compliance
- Personal brand trust

The goal is simple:

Slow down before signing anything you do not understand.

## Core Rule

Do not sign under pressure.

A serious sponsor should allow time for review.

A responsible partner should want the athlete to understand the agreement.

If someone rushes you, pressures you, or says you do not need to ask questions, that is a warning sign.

## Red Flag 1: No Written Agreement

If a sponsor promises payment but refuses to put terms in writing, slow down.

A written agreement should explain:

- Who is involved
- What the athlete must do
- What the sponsor must pay
- When payment happens
- How content can be used
- How long the deal lasts
- Whether either side can cancel

A handshake can start a conversation.

It should not replace clear terms.

## Red Flag 2: Unclear Payment Terms

Do not accept a deal if payment is vague.

Ask:

- How much will I be paid?
- When will I be paid?
- How will I be paid?
- Is payment cash, check, digital transfer, product, service, or a mix?
- Is there a deposit?
- Is payment due before or after posting?
- Who do I contact if payment is late?
- Will tax forms be provided?

If the sponsor cannot explain payment clearly, do not move forward without help.

## Red Flag 3: Lifetime Rights to Your Image

Be careful if a sponsor wants to use your name, photo, video, voice, signature, or content forever.

Ask:

- How long can the sponsor use my image?
- Where can they use it?
- Can they use it in ads?
- Can they edit it?
- Can they keep using it after the deal ends?
- Can I revoke permission?
- Am I being paid enough for those rights?

Lifetime usage should not be given away casually.

## Red Flag 4: Broad Usage Rights

Some contracts may say the sponsor can use your image in any format, anywhere, forever.

That may include:

- Ads
- Websites
- Billboards
- Merchandise
- Social media
- Paid campaigns
- Email marketing
- Third-party promotions
- Future campaigns you never approved

If the language is too broad, ask for limits.

## Red Flag 5: Exclusivity Without Fair Payment

Exclusivity means you may be blocked from working with similar sponsors.

Example:

A restaurant deal may block you from working with other restaurants.

A clothing deal may block you from wearing or promoting other brands.

A gym deal may block you from training elsewhere publicly.

Ask:

- What category is restricted?
- How long does it last?
- What businesses are included?
- What businesses are excluded?
- Am I being paid enough for the restriction?
- Does this block future opportunities?
- Does this conflict with team or school sponsors?

Do not give exclusivity away for a small deal unless you fully understand the tradeoff.

## Red Flag 6: Pressure to Hide the Deal

Never accept a deal that requires you to hide it from:

- Your school
- Your compliance office
- Your coach
- Your parent or guardian, where appropriate
- Your advisor
- Your accountant or tax preparer
- Your attorney, if you have one

A sponsor who wants secrecy may be creating risk.

NIL deals may need to be reported according to school rules.

## Red Flag 7: Use of School Logos, Uniforms, or Facilities Without Approval

Be careful with content involving:

- School logos
- Team uniforms
- Stadiums
- Arenas
- Practice facilities
- Locker rooms
- Team marks
- Mascots
- Official school phrases
- Game footage

Many schools have rules about using marks, facilities, uniforms, and team branding in NIL content.

Always check before posting.

## Red Flag 8: Products or Services That Create Reputation Risk

Some businesses may not fit your values, your age, your school, your team, or your long-term goals.

Ask:

- Do I understand this product?
- Would I use this product?
- Would I be proud to promote this publicly?
- Could this hurt my reputation?
- Could this hurt my team?
- Could this hurt my school?
- Could this create eligibility or compliance problems?
- Could this affect future employers, coaches, sponsors, or family trust?

Not all money is good money.

## Red Flag 9: Confusing Contract Language

If the agreement uses language you do not understand, slow down.

Watch for terms like:

- Perpetual
- Irrevocable
- Worldwide
- Royalty-free
- Transferable
- Assignable
- In perpetuity
- Full ownership
- Exclusive rights
- Liquidated damages
- Indemnification
- Non-disparagement
- Confidentiality
- Work made for hire

These words may have serious consequences.

Ask for help before signing.

## Red Flag 10: Penalties That Are Too Harsh

Some agreements may include penalties if you miss a deadline, cancel, fail to post, or work with another sponsor.

Ask:

- What happens if I am injured?
- What happens if my coach changes my schedule?
- What happens if the game time changes?
- What happens if the sponsor cancels?
- What happens if I cannot attend?
- What happens if the platform removes the post?
- What happens if I accidentally miss a deliverable?

Penalties should be fair and reasonable.

## Red Flag 11: Sponsor Wants Account Control

Do not give sponsors direct control over your personal social media accounts.

Be careful if a sponsor asks for:

- Your password
- Login access
- Full posting control
- Ability to delete content
- Ability to message followers
- Ability to run ads from your account
- Access to private messages

Your account is part of your brand.

Protect it.

## Red Flag 12: Payment Only in Exposure

Exposure does not pay taxes.

Exposure does not create a budget.

Exposure does not replace fair compensation.

Some early opportunities may be unpaid community service or volunteer work, but athletes should clearly understand the difference between:

- Paid NIL work
- Volunteer service
- Free publicity
- Product exchange
- Discount exchange
- Long-term relationship building

Do not let a business call unpaid promotion a partnership if the value only flows one way.

## Red Flag 13: No Clear Deliverables

A deal should clearly state what the athlete must do.

Examples:

- One Instagram post
- Two Instagram stories
- One TikTok video
- One appearance for one hour
- One autograph session
- One youth clinic
- One business spotlight
- One game-week promotion

Avoid vague language like:

- Promote us sometimes
- Be our face
- Support the brand
- Post when you can
- Help us grow
- Represent us everywhere

Clear deliverables prevent conflict.

## Red Flag 14: No End Date

Every agreement should have a clear end date.

Ask:

- When does the deal begin?
- When does the deal end?
- Do usage rights continue after the deal?
- Does exclusivity continue after the deal?
- Can the agreement renew automatically?
- Can either side cancel?

No end date can create long-term problems.

## Red Flag 15: Token, DAO, or Crypto Claims That Promise Profit

If a deal involves tokens, crypto, NFTs, DAOs, or Web3 participation, be extra careful.

Do not trust claims like:

- Guaranteed profit
- Guaranteed pump
- Guaranteed return
- Passive income
- Risk-free rewards
- Guaranteed NIL payouts
- Guaranteed token appreciation
- Guaranteed scholarship support
- Buy now before it is too late

The AND1 DAO utility is education, financial literacy, participation, social media marketing, contributor recognition, and community coordination.

It is not a promise of profit.

## Red Flag 16: Sponsor Does Not Respect Compliance

A responsible sponsor should understand that athletes must follow school rules.

Be careful if a sponsor says:

- You do not need to report this.
- Your school will never know.
- Everyone does it.
- Just post it.
- Do not ask compliance.
- We can use the logo anyway.
- This does not count as NIL.

If the sponsor dismisses compliance, slow down.

## Red Flag 17: You Feel Unsure

If something feels wrong, pause.

Being unsure does not mean you are unprofessional.

It means you are paying attention.

Ask:

- What do I not understand?
- Who can help me review this?
- What is the worst-case scenario?
- Am I being rushed?
- Does this fit my values?
- Is the money worth the risk?

## Before Signing Checklist

Before signing, confirm:

- I understand the sponsor.
- I understand the deliverables.
- I understand payment.
- I understand the timeline.
- I understand usage rights.
- I understand exclusivity.
- I understand cancellation terms.
- I understand school reporting rules.
- I understand social media disclosure requirements.
- I understand possible tax responsibilities.
- I have asked questions.
- I have saved a copy of the agreement.
- I am not being pressured.
- I am comfortable with this deal being public.

## Questions to Ask a Trusted Advisor

Ask:

- What does this agreement actually require me to do?
- Can the sponsor use my image after the deal ends?
- Is exclusivity included?
- Are payment terms clear?
- Are there penalties?
- Could this affect eligibility?
- Could this affect taxes?
- Could this hurt my reputation?
- Should I negotiate any part of this?
- Should I walk away?
