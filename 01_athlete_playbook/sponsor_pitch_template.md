# Sponsor Pitch Template

## Purpose

This template helps HBCU athletes, alumni mentors, and AND1 DAO contributors contact potential NIL sponsors professionally.

A good sponsor pitch should be:

- Clear
- Respectful
- Short
- Specific
- Community-minded
- Compliance-aware
- Easy to respond to

The goal is not to beg for a deal.

The goal is to show a sponsor how partnership with an HBCU athlete can create value for both sides.

## Before You Pitch

Before contacting a sponsor, complete:

- Personal brand statement
- Media kit
- Social media audit
- Sponsor fit review
- School NIL reporting process
- Basic pricing or package ideas
- Financial tracking system

Do not pitch every business.

Pitch businesses that fit your values, audience, school, and community.

## Sponsor Research Checklist

Before reaching out, answer:

Business name:

Owner / manager name:

Location:

Website:

Social media:

What do they sell?

Who do they serve?

Are they connected to the HBCU community?

Are they alumni-owned?

Have they supported athletes or schools before?

Why would this partnership make sense?

What could I help them promote?

## Short DM Pitch

Use this for Instagram, X, Facebook, or LinkedIn.

Hello [Name],

My name is [Athlete Name], and I am a [sport/position] at [School]. I’m reaching out because I respect what [Business Name] is doing in the community.

I am building NIL partnerships with local businesses that support HBCU athletes, education, and community impact. I would love to explore a simple collaboration where I help promote your business through social media, campus visibility, or a community activation.

I can send over my media kit if you are open to learning more.

Thank you,  
[Athlete Name]

## Sponsor Pitch Checklist

Before sending a pitch, confirm:

- I know the sponsor’s name.
- I understand what the business does.
- I can explain why the partnership fits.
- I have a media kit ready.
- I understand my school’s NIL reporting process.
- I have a basic idea of deliverables.
- I know how I will track income.
- I am not promising anything I cannot deliver.
- I am communicating professionally.

## Final Word

A sponsor pitch is not begging.

It is business communication.

You are offering attention, trust, story, content, community connection, and representation.

Approach sponsors with respect.

Protect your name.

Know your value.

Follow the rules.

Track the money.

Build the relationship.
