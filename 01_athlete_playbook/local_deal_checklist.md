# Local Deal Checklist

## Purpose

This checklist helps HBCU athletes evaluate local NIL opportunities before accepting a deal.

Local NIL deals can be powerful because they connect athletes with businesses that already care about the school, the community, and the athlete’s story.

But every deal still needs structure.

A handshake is not enough.

A post is not enough.

A promise is not enough.

The athlete should understand the sponsor, the deliverables, the payment, the timeline, the rights, and the compliance requirements before moving forward.

## Sponsor Fit

Before accepting the deal, answer:

Does this business fit my values?

Does this business fit my audience?

Does this business support my school or community?

Would I promote this business even if people I respect saw the post?

Would my coaches, teammates, family, or school be comfortable with this partnership?

Does this business sell something I understand?

Does this business create any eligibility, reputation, or compliance risk?

## Usage Rights

The athlete should understand how the sponsor can use their name, image, likeness, content, or post.

Can the sponsor repost the athlete’s content?

Can the sponsor use the athlete’s photo on its website?

Can the sponsor use the athlete’s image in ads?

Can the sponsor use the athlete’s image after the deal ends?

How long can the sponsor use the content?

Can the sponsor edit the content?

Can the sponsor use the athlete’s school name, logo, uniform, or team marks?

Has school policy been reviewed for marks, logos, uniforms, and facilities?

## Exclusivity

Exclusivity means the athlete may be blocked from working with similar businesses.

Does the deal include exclusivity?

If yes, what category is restricted?

How long does exclusivity last?

Is the athlete being paid enough for that restriction?

Does exclusivity block future opportunities?

Does exclusivity conflict with school, team, or existing sponsor rules?

## Compliance Review

Before accepting, confirm:

I reviewed my school’s NIL policy.

I know whether I must report the deal.

I know where to report the deal.

I know when to report the deal.

I know whether the deal needs approval before posting.

I know whether I can use school logos, uniforms, facilities, or team marks.

I know whether this sponsor category is allowed.

I know whether the platform disclosure rules apply.

I know whether the content needs to say sponsored, ad, paid partnership, or similar.

## Social Media Disclosure

Sponsor content should be clear.

Possible disclosure language may include:

- Paid partnership with [Business Name]
- Sponsored by [Business Name]
- Thank you to [Business Name] for supporting HBCU athletes
- NIL partnership with [Business Name]
- Advertisement / ad, where required

Athletes should check school, platform, and applicable disclosure rules.

## Reputation Review

Before accepting, ask:

Could this deal embarrass me later?

Could this deal hurt my family, school, team, or community reputation?

Could this deal make people question my judgment?

Am I comfortable explaining this deal publicly?

Do I actually support the business?

Does this sponsor respect athletes?

Would I still want this content online in one year?

## Contract Questions

If there is a written agreement, review:

Who are the parties?

What does the athlete have to do?

What does the sponsor have to pay?

When does payment happen?

How long does the agreement last?

How can either side cancel?

Who owns the content?

How can the sponsor use the athlete’s image?

Is there exclusivity?

Are there penalties?

Is there confidentiality language?

Is there a dispute process?

Is the language clear?

## Red Flags

Stop and ask for help if:

- The sponsor refuses to put terms in writing.
- The sponsor pressures the athlete to sign immediately.
- The sponsor wants lifetime image rights.
- The sponsor wants the athlete to hide the deal.
- The sponsor does not explain payment clearly.
- The sponsor wants school marks used without approval.
- The sponsor offers exposure instead of fair value.
- The sponsor asks the athlete to promote something risky.
- The agreement is confusing.
- The athlete does not understand the deal.
- The deal seems too good to be true.
- The sponsor wants control over the athlete’s account.
- The sponsor delays payment without explanation.

## Local Deal Approval Workflow

Step 1: Athlete identifies sponsor.

Step 2: Athlete reviews sponsor fit.

Step 3: Athlete defines deliverables.

Step 4: Athlete confirms payment.

Step 5: Athlete reviews usage rights.

Step 6: Athlete checks compliance process.

Step 7: Athlete asks trusted advisor or mentor if unsure.

Step 8: Athlete reports deal to school if required.

Step 9: Athlete completes deliverables.

Step 10: Athlete tracks income.

Step 11: Athlete saves agreement and screenshots.

Step 12: Athlete follows up professionally.
