# Athlete NIL 101

## Purpose

This guide introduces HBCU student-athletes to the basics of NIL.

NIL stands for:

- Name
- Image
- Likeness

NIL allows student-athletes to receive compensation for approved activities involving their identity, public presence, content, and influence.

This may include:

- Social media posts
- Autograph signings
- Appearances
- Camps
- Clinics
- Commercials
- Brand partnerships
- Podcast interviews
- Product promotions
- Local business campaigns
- Community events
- Merchandise collaborations

## What NIL Is

NIL is the ability of an athlete to benefit from the use of their personal brand.

A personal brand may include:

- Your name
- Your photo
- Your face
- Your voice
- Your story
- Your signature
- Your social media presence
- Your athletic identity
- Your reputation
- Your campus influence
- Your community influence

NIL is not only for famous athletes.

NIL can work for athletes with strong local trust, clear identity, consistent content, and strong community relationships.

## What NIL Is Not

NIL is not:

- A salary from the school
- A guarantee of payment
- A guarantee of deals
- A replacement for scholarship rules
- A reason to ignore compliance
- A promise of professional sports success
- A license to promote anything without consequences
- A shortcut around school, conference, state, federal, NCAA, or platform rules

NIL is opportunity.

Opportunity still requires preparation.

## Why NIL Matters for HBCU Athletes

HBCU athletes often carry strong cultural value.

They represent:

- Historic institutions
- Family pride
- Alumni communities
- Campus culture
- Regional identity
- Black excellence
- Local businesses
- Youth inspiration
- Community leadership

That value can become responsible NIL opportunity when athletes are prepared.

## The NIL Readiness Formula

A prepared NIL athlete should have:

```text
Identity + Story + Audience + Trust + Compliance + Financial Literacy = NIL Readiness

Step 1: Know Your Identity

Before chasing deals, answer:

* Who am I?
* What do I represent?
* What sport do I play?
* What position do I play?
* What school do I represent?
* What is my hometown?
* What is my major?
* What is my story?
* What values do I want attached to my name?
* What brands fit me?
* What brands do not fit me?

Step 2: Know Your Audience

Your audience may include:

* Teammates
* Classmates
* Coaches
* Alumni
* Family
* Local fans
* Youth athletes
* Churches
* Community groups
* Local businesses
* Other athletes
* Social media followers

You do not need millions of followers.

You need trust and alignment.

Step 3: Know Your Value

Your value may come from:

* Athletic performance
* Campus visibility
* Community trust
* Social media engagement
* Leadership
* Personality
* Work ethic
* Storytelling
* Local relevance
* Alumni pride
* Event participation
* Sponsor fit

Do not reduce your value to follower count only.

Step 4: Know the Rules

Before accepting or promoting a deal, athletes should check:

* School NIL policy
* Athletic department reporting process
* Conference rules
* State law
* NCAA rules
* Team rules
* Platform rules
* Sponsor disclosure rules

If you are unsure, ask your compliance office before moving forward.

Step 5: Know the Money

NIL income may create responsibilities.

Athletes should track:

* Who paid them
* How much they were paid
* When they were paid
* What they did for payment
* What expenses were related to the deal
* Whether taxes may be owed
* Whether a form was provided
* Whether the agreement was written

A deal is not complete until the money is tracked.

Common NIL Deal Types

Social Media Post

The athlete posts content promoting a business, product, service, or event.

Appearance

The athlete attends an event, store opening, youth camp, community event, or sponsor activation.

Autograph Signing

The athlete signs items for fans or sponsor customers.

Camp or Clinic

The athlete helps teach younger athletes.

Sponsored Video

The athlete creates short-form video content for a sponsor.

Ambassador Relationship

The athlete represents a brand over a longer period of time.

Affiliate or Discount Code

The athlete receives compensation or credit when people use a code or link.

Merchandise Collaboration

The athlete collaborates on clothing, posters, digital products, or other merchandise where allowed.

NIL Deal Checklist

Before accepting a deal, ask:

* Who is the sponsor?
* What do they want me to do?
* How much will I be paid?
* When will I be paid?
* How will I be paid?
* How long can they use my name, image, or likeness?
* Can they reuse my content?
* Is there exclusivity?
* Can I work with similar brands?
* Do I need to post disclosures?
* Does my school need to approve or receive notice?
* Could this hurt my eligibility?
* Could this hurt my reputation?
* Do I understand the agreement?

Red Flags

Slow down if a sponsor:

* Pressures you to sign immediately
* Refuses to put terms in writing
* Wants lifetime rights to your image
* Uses confusing language
* Wants you to hide the deal
* Offers exposure instead of fair value
* Asks you to promote something risky
* Does not explain payment terms
* Requires exclusivity without fair compensation
* Wants control over your personal accounts
